## The readout

`ds.y[:, 0]`: the per-trial response at the single layer 41, the array `analyze_dataset` scored, used untransformed
(`Trials.build` only sorts by concept and pads). 64 concepts x 6 carriers x 7 levels x D = 4 draws = 10,752 trials per
dataset, 1,536 per dose level. The synthetic design has one readout per trial; the lens projection that the real-model
pipeline carries as a second readout has no synthetic counterpart, so there is no second-readout column.

## The tests and the declared rules

Declared before the batteries ran (constants at the top of the script, unchanged after):

- Dose levels: `models.LEVELS = (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8)`. **Tested levels 2, 3, 4**: the near-threshold levels, where the
  declared mixture base occupies the high state with probability A(k) = sigmoid(1.5 (k - 3)) = 0.18, 0.50, 0.82
  (A = 0.047 at k = 1, 0.989 at k = 6); the graded base's logistic mean has its midpoint at k = 3 as well. The readout is
  pooled over concepts and carriers at each level, as the literature pools over prompts or items at a fixed level.
- **T1, Hartigan's dip test** (Hartigan & Hartigan 1985; `diptest` 0.12.0, `diptest.diptest(x)`, interpolated
  p-value table). Call: any tested level with p < 0.05 / 3 (**Bonferroni over the three levels**). Sensitivity reading
  beside it: all seven levels, p < 0.05 / 7 (`dip_call_all`).
- **T2, mixture BIC** (`sklearn.mixture.GaussianMixture`, `covariance_type="full"`, one against two components,
  `n_init=5`, `random_state` from the dataset seed and the level index). delta BIC = BIC(1) - BIC(2). Call: the largest
  delta BIC over the three tested levels **> 10** (Kass & Raftery 1995, "very strong"); the **> 0** reading beside it.
  No multiplicity adjustment is defined for BIC; the any-level rule over the three declared levels is the rule, and the
  seven-level variant is written beside it (`gmm_call_strong_all`).
- **T3, Rahbar's aggregate ignition index** (arXiv 2608.05160 v1, read in full on 25 Oct: s4.1 eq. 3 and Definition 1,
  s6.4, s7 C1). The index is the fitted steepness beta of the four-parameter logistic
  f(x) = ymin + (ymax - ymin) / (1 + exp(-beta (x - x0))), fitted by nonlinear least squares (`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`,
  Levenberg-Marquardt), initialised at x0 = the first level at or above the midpoint of max and min and beta from the
  local slope across that level scaled by the range, and capped at beta = 300 (the paper's numerical ceiling, also
  applied when the fit does not converge). The paper's own x axis is layer depth (signal strength indexes separate fits);
  this battery has one layer, so the form is fitted to the **mean readout against the dose level** (beta per unit of k).
  The paper defines **no verdict threshold** (high beta = abrupt, low = graded build-up; a delta-AICc comparison of
  linear, sigmoid and step curves is a fit-quality control, not a two-state verdict), so T3 reports the distribution of
  beta per setting (median, quartiles, min, max, share capped) and makes no call. The transition width ln(81)/beta of the
  paper is a monotone transform and is not tabulated.
- **T4, this paper's procedure**: the saved `selection_decision` of every row, no refit.
- **Rates**: per setting, calls over datasets with the exact one-sided 95% Clopper-Pearson bound the article uses:
  the upper bound on graded settings (a bound on the false two-state rate), the lower bound on mixture settings (a bound
  on the detection rate). 0/1000 gives 0.003; 50/50 gives 0.942; 49/50 gives 0.909.

A test that reports two states on graded data with item heterogeneity is measured doing so, nothing more; no statement
here says that a published result is false.

What each test reports on these generators, in its own terms: the dip test, pooled over 64 heterogeneous concepts at one
level, finds no departure from unimodality on any graded dataset and on 18 of the 600 mixture datasets at these effect
sizes; the two-component Gaussian mixture is preferred by BIC wherever the pooled readout is not Gaussian, which the
graded generators with item heterogeneity or skew produce, and at the two larger gains under every mixture generator;
the steepness of the mean curve does not separate the two generator classes, whose mean curves share the same logistic
dose dependence by construction, and the index's source defines no threshold to call. The paper's procedure answers a
different question (which family of conditional densities scores better out of sample, per concept), so the columns are
not ranked against each other.

## Runtime and environment

Graded battery 12,000 datasets: 124.2 s on 12 joblib workers (0.010 s per dataset: regeneration, seven levels of dip
test and two mixture fits, one logistic fit); mixture battery 600 datasets: 6.7 s. Tie check: about 15 s wall per battery
(five rows in parallel). The whole job's compute, excluding the background full-procedure reproductions, was under
three minutes on the 16-core Mac. Environment `requirements_alt_tests.txt`: python 3.12.14, numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1,
pandas 3.0.5, joblib 1.6.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1, diptest 0.12.0, jax 0.11.1 (imported by the historical models.py; no
fit is run by the battery), arm64. The tie check and the full reproductions ran in `t1_access/.venv` (jax fits).
