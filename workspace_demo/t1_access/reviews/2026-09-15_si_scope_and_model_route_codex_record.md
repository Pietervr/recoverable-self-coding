## Q3. A pilot Delta is permissible, with a specific reporting contract

Section 5 explicitly permits pilot model-comparison numbers while withholding
outcome decisions. The failed coverage audit does not make the computed score
undefined: it prevents treating the associated interval as calibrated evidence.
Numerical failures and changes of fitting policy still have to be reported.

Prefer the pooled point estimate plus all 16 concept scores, grouped by the
eight families, and the eight descriptive family means. Report the frozen
decoder's held-out accuracy and dose placement first. Label spread as observed
spread; with only two concepts per family, a family interval carries very little
independent information. Do not display an unvalidated bootstrap interval as a
95% population CI, or rescue that interpretation by calling it “descriptive”.
If a resampling diagnostic is useful, put its exact conditional meaning and
known coverage limitation in the supplement. No p-value, H1 verdict or selected
best layer is needed for A.

There is an additional implementation decision before scoring. Section 8.1
specifies five outer/four inner folds for 64 CONF concepts; `validate_pilot.py`
produces readouts and instrument diagnostics, not a pilot family-comparison
contract. `analyze.stratified_folds` can allocate the 16 PILOT concepts using
round-robin family offsets: this is not an automatic code error. But two
concepts per family cannot put every family in each of five test folds, and
inner training samples are much smaller than in CONF. Do not present this as
the same validated design at lower cost.

Before reading the pilot comparison, save its fold memberships/seeds, fitting
policy, predictor, layer summary, failure handling and training-size estimand.
Using the existing five/four pooled algorithm descriptively is possible if
the sparse family representation is stated and the folds are checked without
looking at scores. Changing the fold counts or using CAL-trained density
predictors is also a different pilot estimand and must be declared. Never
choose the scoring design from whichever gives the desired sign. The single-layer
64-concept audit does not validate any of these 16-concept intervals.

Suggested result wording, with actual values inserted only after that contract:

> On 16 held-out pilot concepts, the specified exploratory scoring procedure
> gave a mean workspace-band log-score difference of [value] nat per trial.
> The figure shows every concept and family summary. This pilot informed
> instrument development; its score does not adjudicate H1, and no calibrated
> confidence statement or population claim is made from it.

Include the pilot regardless of its sign if this reporting route is chosen;
record any permitted dose change and all development uses. Keep pilot concepts
out of CONF. Repeated captures of a deterministic prompt, trials within a
concept, and layers are not independent model subjects. A can make the proposed

## Q4. B as proposed is not a defensible confirmatory route

This is not a problem cured by finding USD 1,100. Three separate requirements
were weakened in the proposal without justification:

1. **Least-favourable first is an order, not a subset certificate.** There is no
   established stochastic domination showing that three or four settings bound
   the rest for the amended fitter and interval. Old passing settings can fail
   under a new procedure. The prior review required every retained null.
2. **Unresolved omega-2 is unresolved.** If it remains in the claimed validation
   domain, its unresolved target prevents all-setting coverage passage. Stopping
   its expenditure is sensible; calling that a pass is not. Removing it after
   finding it inconvenient needs an explicit, scientifically justified change
   of domain, independent of CONF, and sharply limited claims. It does not
   validate the omitted regime or the original scope.
3. **Power unassessed is not the preregistered underpowered fallback.** Section 10
   selects D through power at 0.01 nat and permits the fallback after measured
   power below 0.8 at D=8. Section 9's label does not authorize skipping that
   work. A power-unassessed test can be prospectively designed in principle,
   with a fixed D and calibrated type-I error, but that is another amendment;
   a non-rejection cannot become evidence of absence, and calibration failures
   remain disqualifying.

For the existing validation domain, retain the **twelve labelled grid settings**:
M2B; M2H tau = 0, 0.5, 1, 2; M2S omega = 0, 0.5, 1, 2; M2K alpha = 0, 1, 3.
Section 10 additionally requires CAL/PILOT-fitted null parameters and validation
of the actual correlated workspace-band statistic. Distributionally identical
zero-effect generators might share validation only after demonstrating equivalence
of the complete data law and analysis policy. Similar means or nearly identical
saved arrays do not establish that equivalence. I endorse no omitted settings
on the evidence here.

The earlier proposed design remains a **minimum reviewed design for one locked
procedure, one D, one synthetic layer and twelve nulls**, not a universal minimum
sample size or completed validation:

| Attempted datasets per setting | Role | Pass boundary if every interval is usable |
|---:|---|---|
| 100, 200 | Failure-only checks | No passage |
| 400 | First eligible terminal check | At least 373 intervals contain the independent target interval; at most 15 false mixture calls |
| 1,000 | Sole predeclared optional extension | At least 920 contain the target interval; at most 48 false mixture calls |

Use the full prior record for failure boundaries, actual usable denominators,
minimum 400 usable intervals, stopping and error allocation. The targets need
independent, policy-matched intervals with simultaneous 99% coverage; a fixed
1,000-row reference bank is not guaranteed to achieve this, particularly at
omega-2. The old-policy banks do not validate a new fitter. Passing all nulls
uses the prior intersection-union argument, not an unnecessary factor of twelve
on every pass test. Additional settings, repeated adoption attempts and further
band/D claims need an explicitly scoped error budget; the twelve-setting failure
cutoffs cannot simply be copied onto a larger campaign.

The previous record already calculated how often near-threshold procedures
remain unresolved. At true coverage 0.91, even a known target gives only 6.44%
chance of meeting the 400-row coverage boundary. No extension or relaxed
criterion may be chosen after seeing an inconvenient result. All-null single-layer
passage still leaves alternative coverage, recovery, power and band validation.

is not. It is a historical blended cost for ordinary one-layer datasets under
the old fitting/fixed-score-bootstrap policy, including wasted gain stages.
It is neither a current spot quote nor a replacement-interval price.

| Arithmetic using that historical unit | Result | What it leaves out |
|---|---:|---|
| 4 × 1,000 reference rows + 4 × 400 validation rows | USD 1,120 | Eight other grid settings, fitted nulls, interval refits, amended starts, band and remaining stages |
| 12 × 1,000 reference rows + 12 × 400 validation rows | USD 3,360 | Same omissions except the remaining grid settings; reference adequacy still unproved |

Even the second illustrative ordinary-fit total exceeds the standing USD 3,000
further-spend cap, before considering its already-used portion. It is not a
forecast or a lower bound: different machines/spot rates may change it. The
ledger reports approximately USD 84.8 for the 1,000-row omega-2 spot bank
(about USD 0.085 per ordinary dataset); that shows why measured stage prices
matter, not that every revised dataset has that price.

The old Mac benchmark is 15.3 core-minutes per refit resample per layer.
Consequently B=100 costs 25.5 core-hours per dataset-layer; B=200 costs 51.
Four nulls × 400 datasets × B=100 already means **40,800 core-hours** for
those refits alone. At the *historical* USD 0.48 per Mac-core-hour equivalent,
that is USD 19,584 before banks or other stages. This is a scale illustration,
not a current cloud estimate. Stronger starts require a new benchmark; JOB D's
old loaded-PC ratio is not a universal multiplier.

The timeline also omits the observed-data interval. One 35-layer workspace-band
interval costs, under that same old benchmark, about 893 core-hours at B=100
or 1,785 at B=200: ideal 11-worker times of 3.38 or 6.76 days respectively,
before failures, overhead or competing jobs. R2, other bands and conditions add
work. The reported 1.5-hour analysis is ordinary point fitting over 63 layers;
it cannot price refitting intervals. These products were checked with Decimal
arithmetic only; the earlier binomial audit was not rerun.

Re-price reference, validation, alternatives, band, capture and final-analysis
stages separately after the procedure is fixed, including extensions and remaining
budget. The approximately USD 2,800 balance is the owner's dated ledger report,
not a fresh billing measurement by this review. Free compute reduces cash cost,
but capacity and wall time remain unmeasured for the final policy. B is not
scientifically impossible at every budget; **B as written is insufficient at
any budget**. A fully amended, validated route is a different plan.

## Q5. Before CONF, and the cheaper development candidate

Keep the current protocol's stronger boundary: freeze and register before CONF
captures, not merely before someone displays the result. Required in sequence:

1. Close the two-reader stimulus audit, capture-machine choice and exact code,
   tokenizer, weights, lens and runtime provenance. Apply the qualifying parity
   and suffix gates on that path. R084 owns these records; this review does not
   reopen or replace its endpoint review.
2. Freeze CAL decoders/scaling; complete the PILOT accuracy and dose checks and
   record permitted changes. Supply the fitted null parameters and cross-layer
   structure needed by validation. `validate_pilot.py` currently reports a mean
   raw score correlation: that scalar, which mixes dose/concept signal, is not
   by itself the residual cross-layer generator required by §10. Its saved
   scores can support that later estimation step.
3. Commit the complete revised scientific/statistical contract: estimand, model
   bounds and floors, starts at both fitting sizes, seeds, recovery/failure rules,
   predictor, D, layer grid/summary, interval or test, validation domain and
   counts, reference method, stopping and decision language. Settle selection
   before untouched validation and obtain the scoped review the plan requires.
4. Complete the validation applicable to the actual claimed result, including
   band dependence and the protocol's alternative/recovery/power work unless
   explicitly and defensibly amended. Account separately for the R2 comparison
   and target bridge. A single-layer R1 pass is not a certificate for those.
5. Pin the v2 manifest and reporting/interpretation rules. Report the content
   diagnostics before interpreting H1. R1 alone supports at most a coherence
   readout result; “discontinuous access” needs the declared bridge. H3 has its
   own state/matching/control/positive-control conditions and does not follow
   automatically from H1. Preserve all assay-failure and inconclusive outcomes.

For cheaper development, I favour screening a **fixed one-sided critical-value
test on the held-out band score**. In schematic form, after development fixes
the statistic and threshold, call mixture support only if the valid statistic
T exceeds max(0, c), with ties and failures specified. Choose c conservatively
against the declared graded domain; then use independent full-procedure null
datasets to validate its false-call rate. Evaluate alternatives to learn whether
it has useful sensitivity. A finite grid supports that finite domain, not a
universal graded-class guarantee.

This avoids constructing a confidence interval for the procedure's mean and
the nested refits/reference-mean estimation needed for its coverage. It does
not avoid fitting the original predictive comparison on each synthetic dataset,
nor actual-band validation. It is a different primary rule and needs an explicit
amendment. Non-rejection is inconclusive unless a separate graded-support rule
is specified and calibrated. It supplies neither a 95% effect interval nor
automatic validation of H1-T or H3.

Saved d4v12b per-concept scores can screen this idea for the **original one-layer
procedure**, using declared development splits and considering sensitivity as
well as FPR. They cannot replay stronger starts, reconstruct omitted fit
variation, supply missing alternatives or manufacture genuine cross-layer
dependence. Independent seeds under the final procedure remain necessary.
Permutation of dose labels is not a ready replacement: dose dependence is
allowed under the graded null, so that permutation tests a different null.
Nothing in this suggestion authorizes a screening run now.

## Q6. Principal referee hazards and final corrections

**Novelty needs a narrower sentence.** Gurnee et al. already report sharp
responses to interpolated concept embeddings and bimodal projection shares
across prompts at maximal ambiguity (§4.1.1 and Appendix A.15). The Dehaene–Naccache
commentary proposes stronger ignition tests but also acknowledges the added
all-or-none evidence. A general “first two-state LLM” claim would be unsafe.
The plausible contribution is the declared natural-text population, explicit
graded competitors, audited predictive inference and proposed state-conditional
causal test. This is a focused source comparison, not an exhaustive novelty
search. Sources: [Gurnee et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15495v1#S4.SS1.SSS1),
[Dehaene–Naccache commentary, pp. 7–8](https://unicog.org/wp_2025/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Dehaene-and-Naccache-Workspace-commentary-on-Gurnee-Lindsey-June-2026.pdf).

**The inference chain must stay short and conditional.** Predictive mixture
support does not demonstrate latent switching, dynamical bistability, conscious
experience or a shared human/model mechanism. With one deterministic checkpoint,
variability belongs to the declared stimulus population. The R1 endpoint decoder
can distinguish coherence or other packet properties without identifying the
target; R2/content controls and state-conditional intervention matter precisely
because of that ambiguity. A positive pilot must not bypass them.

**Avoid an unfinished-project narrative.** Lead with the human reproduction and
the completed audit's scientific consequence; present the transfer compactly.
The gain troubleshooting, fitter recipe competition, compute ledger and Melcon
development do not each need a Results subsection. Conversely, zero FPR without
measured power can describe a conservative or uninformative rule: make the
coverage failure, unknown power and lack of band validation explicit. At omega-2,
show uncertainty in the target rather than asserting precisely known 22% coverage
or removing its extreme observation from the mean.

**Protect the human result from overstatement.** Retain the passive analysis,
the post-result choice of active as the transfer reference, optimizer-boundary
sensitivity and the distinction between predictive scores and Bayesian model
evidence. Descriptive protected exceedance probabilities do not become calibrated
p-values through a multiple-window threshold. The cross-substrate numerical
gain scales are also different estimands; an equal number in nats is not an
equal biological or model effect.

**Reconcile the retained text with the protocol at revision.** Methods currently
states a 1e-3 nat quadrature pass rule for generating, fitted and cross-fitted
parameters together; preregistration §7.4 permits 5e-3 for cross-fitted parameters.
Appendix R3 still says unqualified argmax, whereas the September 15 amendment
uses the frozen 128-token closed set, with open-vocabulary rank/argmax secondary.
Remove or update the dated status box, orphaned Melcon references and availability
claims when the scope is decided. These are review findings, not edits made here.

**Match every reported result to an accessible exact source.** The published
78-file version-1 DOI is a fixed earlier snapshot; it cannot stand in for the
later omega-2 bank or subsequent results. Before submission, provide the exact
data/code/protocol version for every retained finding and distinguish preprint,
proceedings and new journal content. Preserve the AI-use disclosure. I did not
verify the current Entropy author-policy page because direct access returned
429; this is a reproducibility recommendation, not a claim that the page was read.
