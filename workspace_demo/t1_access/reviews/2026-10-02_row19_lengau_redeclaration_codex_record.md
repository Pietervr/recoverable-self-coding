### A's recovery proof must be feasible and fail closed

The application's saved resample identity/checksum machinery is useful, but its present behavior is weaker than the row's promise:

- Original `run_dataset` executes before bootstrap checkpoint loading and is redone on resume. The quoted 1.8 hours is a generic old benchmark, not a measured amended omega-1 original-fit cost.
- Rejected records increment `n_damaged` and are recomputed; a new identity can choose a fresh filename and redo all work. Conflicting valid duplicate indices use the last record. That does not implement “failed restore holds for diagnosis and never re-buys fits.” Add a committed preflight/guard that checks the intended identity/file/expected completed IDs and refuses mismatches, corruption or conflicting duplicates; distinguish a known interrupted incomplete tail from unexplained damage.
- Records are flushed but have no `fsync`/writer-lock guarantee in this path. Demonstrate the declared durable-write and single-writer strategy on Lustre under real PBS walltime interruption. Existing mocked tests do not supply this evidence.
- Final CSV publication can wait for a whole dataset chunk. Set the slice's task/chunk/checkpoint layout explicitly so a completed dataset is not lost behind a slow neighbour. Save/archive as work finishes; do not rely on a SageMaker mirror that is absent here.

**An uninterrupted full B200 control is itself a scheduling problem if the chain exceeds 144 hours.** It cannot be promised as a single `seriallong` case while asserting that such a case does not fit. For the first engineering proof, I accept a predeclared exact-index **subchain under the unchanged B200 identity** run normally and with deliberate walltime interruption, comparing all completed scientific outputs, followed by completion of the resumed B200 chain if the budget permits. This establishes recovery for the exercised segment, **not full-chain uninterrupted parity**. A full-control claim needs a legal separately specified path and its complete duplicate cost. Do not compare different random datasets as if their results should agree.

Keep B=200 and the B-sized stratified index arrays from the outset; B=5/50 is a different identity, not a reusable prefix. A declared segment/stop interface is needed for a scoped subchain; the reviewed generic runner does not expose it. ## 4. T at omega 1: use the range for sensitivity, not an automatic confidence set

The historical reported numbers imply MCSE 0.0112415 and a ±2-MCSE range **[−0.891996,−0.847030]**, calculated from those reported values. The saved 17/20 count's stability over that range supports omega 1's place first. It does not justify T for the amended procedure, and small MCSE does not quantify policy mismatch or numerical bias.

First choose the complete procedure. For the new policy, generate/pin an **independent policy-matched omega-1 reference** with a declared target-estimation/error plan; hashes, retained valid-point rules, seed disjointness, numerical identity and tail/MCSE diagnostics are part of it. Name omega 1's actual source/files/filter separately. The ten final_2026-09-15 shards named in §10.1 belong to the **omega 2 bank**, not omega 1. Old references remain historical/sensitivity evidence unless a predeclared numerical equivalence argument actually establishes the required target compatibility; optimizer/padding amendments cannot simply be relabelled.

For a single standalone futility screen, my preference is a declared **99% reference confidence set** if its mean-interval approximation is defensible under that reference distribution. A proposed form is `mean ± t_(.995,n−1) × MCSE` under justified normal/CLT conditions, with predeclared tail/stability checks and budget exit. Calling it 99% is conditional on those assumptions; changing 2 to 2.58 does not validate a tail-dominated mean, and diagnostics do not prove coverage. If a justified set cannot be supplied, coverage disposition stays unresolved. Do not switch to a median, trim extremes or make the expectation target conditional on convenient successful fits. [Mean-interval assumptions](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/conflimi.htm), [simulation estimands/MCSE](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086).

The proposed **Kmax safeguard is sound conditionally**: if the true target lies in T, its covered count is at most Kmax. Requiring the upper-bound failure at Kmax is therefore conservative. Compute the maximum over all interval endpoints/intervening segments within T, including inclusive endpoint ties, not merely its ends or a coarse grid. Kmax itself is **not a binomial count at a fixed target**; the argument is containment/monotonicity, not an “exact CI for maximum coverage.”

At a fixed true target and iid usable interval cases, the 83/100 cutoff has a one-sided exact upper bound 0.897735; 84 gives 0.905690. The false-futility probability at true coverage 0.90 is about 0.020599, at most the nominal 0.025. With a justified 99% target set, total one-look erroneous-stop probability is bounded conservatively by **0.025+0.01=0.035** (about 0.030599 using the actual discrete cutoff). Declare that combined allowance or allocate a different one prospectively. A roughly 95% ±2-MCSE range does not preserve a 2.5% overall claim. Reusing 83 across later settings/looks supplies no campaign-wide guarantee; stage 3 still needs the committed allocation of errors and fresh go. [Official exact-binomial method](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats._result_classes.BinomTestResult.proportion_ci.html).

Resolve “100 usable” versus “no failed row replaced.” Prefer **100 fixed attempted datasets**: report each method's usable n and failures, recompute its exact bound at that n, and apply 83 only when n=100. Report jointly usable paired n and refit-only/cluster-only/both/neither counts there; do not conflate marginal and joint denominators. A predeclared capped design that attempts cases until 100 usable are obtained would be another explicit choice, never a silent refill. Conditional coverage among usable intervals is distinct from unconditional availability/reliability. Freeze and review the external evaluator before stage 2; it must reject incompatible references and never use the screen's own mean. No slice outcome or “not ruled out” is a coverage pass.
