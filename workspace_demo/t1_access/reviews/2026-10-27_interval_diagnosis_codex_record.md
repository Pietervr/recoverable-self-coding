## Findings, in order of consequence

1. **There is a cheap, identifiable finite-sample variance correction, but it repairs only part of the failure.** Independent calculation reproduces all twelve inclusion rates and all twelve endpoint-inflation results in `diag_fix.out`. Multiplying deviations of the saved endpoints from the point by `sqrt(8/7)` moves eight settings above the 0.90 development floor, leaving four below: threshold `tau=0.5`, 0.849; scale `omega=0.5`, 0.877; `omega=1`, 0.806; `omega=2`, 0.223. The exact factor concerns a **linear mean of fixed scores relative to the usual unbiased within-stratum sample-variance estimate**, not the sampling variance of the complete fitted procedure. It is not a theorem that every refitting interval needs exactly this correction. Sources: `analyze.py:312–338`; independent `sim_results/interval_diag_2026-10-27/codex/reproduced.json`.

2. **“Selection is fixed, so selection switching is not involved” is false at `tau=0.5`.** The graded winner is always M2H, but the mixture winner is not fixed: 644 datasets select only M3V across their five folds, 46 select only M3H, and 310 select both. `diag_interval.py:53–64` tabulates only the graded winners. The observed width deficit is real; its allocation to parameter estimation, mixture selection, fold allocation, or optimizer behaviour is not identified. A near-zero random-effect variance is an unchecked explanation, not a diagnosis. Sources: reproduced `selected_X` counts; `analyze.py:199–251`.

3. **The scale-tail mechanism has strong support, but two claims in the brief overstate it.** Skews of −5.92 and −13.71 and the omega-2 dominance association reproduce. At `omega=1`, however, misses are 216 with the reference below the interval **and four above**, not exclusively one-sided. At `omega=2` all 784 misses are in the former direction. Nor is it true that no width fix can reach the floor: modest inflation fails, but the empirical factors needed to reach 0.90 in this same development bank are about 1.407 and 11.760 times the original endpoint deviations. These are overfit diagnostic factors, not recommended intervals. Sources: `diag_interval.out`; independent `additional.json`; `paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:50–52` already gives the mechanism and its limits.

4. **A full-refit bootstrap has a further small-sample problem beyond the fixed-score `7/8` calculation.** A resample has only 42.009 distinct concepts on average out of 64, 5.251 per stratum; copies are correctly kept together. Thus the effective number of distinct training concepts drops and hard selection is repeated on a substantially different empirical support. The outer fold labels are inherited, not freshly randomized. This is a plausible finite-sample source of bootstrap bias/nonregularity, not proof that the candidate fails. Sources: `analyze.py:114–136, 657–674`; independent resampling calculation in `reproduced.json`.

5. **Deleting one whole family is the wrong jackknife for this design.** Families are fixed strata, with concepts sampled within them. Whole-family deletion measures a different perturbation and removes a part of the target population. Eight balanced deletion groups, each containing one concept from each family, are a defensible cheaper *candidate* for full-refit variance estimation. Eight groups and a `t_7` multiplier do not establish coverage for hard selection, overlapping CV fits, or a rare-event mean. Sources: `simulate.py:81–89`; `analyze.py:312–319`; Q3 below.

6. **The other major measured failures include fitting and the new matched human comparison.** The local fitter audit records missed better training optima despite convergence. The current paper also reports a failed matched-comparison calibration condition, unavailable high-latency alternatives, and latency-profile coverage below 0.90. Repairing the model-side interval does not repair these. Sources: `reviews/pc_audit_2026-09-14/results/audit_fits.md:7–39, 180–190`; `paper:sections/jitter_results.tex:6–10, 28`.

7. **The three narrow claims of the submission survive this review.** The saved results support partial reproduction, the stated generating-family call counts, and failure of the original interval's declared inclusion criterion. This review finds no validated replacement. The main change is a more specific development diagnosis, not permission to interpret held model data. Sources: `paper:sections/introduction.tex:34`; Q5–Q6 below.

### The exact small-stratum calculation

Write `W_h=n_h/C` and let `s_h²` be the within-stratum score variance with denominator `n_h−1`. For independent scores with fixed predictors, the usual stratified estimate is

`V_strat = sum_h W_h² s_h²/n_h`.

The conditional variance of the empirical resampling mean is

`V_boot = sum_h W_h² [(n_h−1)/n_h] s_h²/n_h`.

At eight concepts per stratum, `V_boot=(7/8)V_strat`, with SE ratio 0.935414 and correction 1.069045. Strictly, the empirical-distribution plug-in variance already uses denominator `n_h`; call this a correction relative to the unbiased sample-variance reference, rather than saying it is smaller than every “plug-in” estimator. At this balanced design `V_strat=sum_h s_h²/512`. My median ratios of saved bootstrap SE to its exact conditional formula range from 0.9991 to 1.0011; Monte Carlo variation accounts for the remaining discrepancy. This verifies the resampling arithmetic directly. Pooling all concepts into `sd(delta)/sqrt(64)`, as in Table `tab:unit`, is a different comparison and includes between-stratum sample-mean variation.

For fixed scores, scaling endpoints around the point is algebraically legitimate: the point is the exact bootstrap expectation, and the transformed interval is the percentile interval of `T+c(T*−T)`. It retains asymmetric distances; it is not a normal-theory replacement. It corrects a variance convention, not all percentile-interval coverage error. With unequal stratum sizes there is no universal `sqrt(8/7)` correction.

For the nonlinear refitting statistic, bootstrap expectation need not equal the original point. Scaling around that point also scales the bootstrap bias. The same sampling scheme motivates investigating a finite-sample allowance, but does **not** establish an exact multiplicative correction to the entire refit variance or its endpoints.

## Q2 — Additional causes and qualifications

**Verdict: overlapping-fit covariance, selection nonregularity, finite bootstrap support, interval shape, and target definition all matter. Quadrature remains a bounded-scope numerical question; these saved rows do not show it caused the inclusion failure.**

- **Overlapping CV fits.** Concept draws can be independent while their out-of-fold scores are dependent. Concepts in one outer fold share a fitted predictor; fits in different outer folds share most training data. A fixed-score bootstrap perturbs the empirical losses but never lets one concept change the predictors used to score other concepts. Training-only inner selection prevents outer test leakage; it does not remove these covariances. This is the relevant Bates–Hastie–Tibshirani concern. Their uncertainty-estimation nesting is not supplied merely by this code's inner loop for member selection. Their results motivate an additional variance procedure; they are not a plug-in theorem for this selected joint-score contrast. [Bates, Hastie and Tibshirani, original manuscript](https://tibshirani.su.domains/ftp/NCV.pdf). Code: `analyze.py:199–251`.

- **Hard selection and imperfect optimization.** Argmax selection is discontinuous near member ties; variance components and mixture degeneracies can be nonregular. Successful optimizer termination does not establish the best training solution. `models.py:650–705` invokes recovery when no start converges, not whenever a better basin exists. The saved fitter audit demonstrates the distinction. A smooth-estimator bootstrap or jackknife guarantee cannot be assumed in this setting.

- **Percentile shape and scale estimation are separate.** Basic versus percentile endpoints, bias, skew, random widths, and point–width dependence all affect inclusion. My fixed-score basic interval gives 0.749/0.180 at `omega=1/2`, compared with percentile 0.780/0.216; after F1 it gives 0.765/0.182. Shape changes alone are not an evident rescue. Oracle `SD_rep/median(SE)` inflation uses the same replicate distribution and target one is evaluating. At omega 2 it gives 1.000, which demonstrates that “no width fix works” is too strong but says nothing about implementable uncertainty. Ratios involving median SE do not equal ratios of variance expectations. The paper's RMS-SE observation is compatible with the common narrow intervals and occasional enormous ones (`paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:35`).

- **The empirical bootstrap cannot generate unseen scale extremes.** It can repeat an extreme concept already observed, but it cannot sample a larger scale absent from the dataset. Refitting changes fitted densities but does not remove this support restriction. The expected 42 distinct concepts also changes finite-sample training/selection behaviour. Neither limitation is corrected in general by a Bessel factor. Larger B estimates the same conditional bootstrap law more precisely; it does not add new concept support.

- **The target is design- and algorithm-specific.** `theta_g=E_g(T)` at D=4, C=64, one layer is coherent and is the reference the scripts estimate. It includes finite training sizes, selection, integration, starts and partitions (`paper:sections/theory.tex:42–67`; `methods.tex:110`). It is not generator gain, a risk conditional on one frozen fit, or a design-invariant measure of two states. Increasing D changes a joint concept likelihood divided by `42D`, the fitted predictors and the target. Changing family counts, starts, models, layer summary or clipping likewise requires new targets. A zero score difference is equal predictive performance, not the full generating-family null. This distinction limits what repairing an interval would establish.

- **Randomness needs a matching specification.** `refit_bootstrap` inherits each concept's original outer fold and regenerates inner folds and optimizer streams (`analyze.py:621–626,657–674`). It does not freshly sample the outer partition as the broad prose description could suggest. Whether fixed-fold resampling adequately approximates an estimand averaging over partitions is a question to validate, not an automatic contradiction or proof of failure. A paired fixed-data perturbation of fold/start seeds could locate this contribution without conflating it with new concept draws; no such experiment was run here.

- **Quadrature has been investigated, but not universally certified.** The current implementation is a two-scale trapezoid rule, despite residual GH naming (`models.py:421–460`). The audit's pass rules are 10^−3 nat per concept at generating/fitted parameters and 5×10^−3 at cross-fits (`audit_quadrature.py:4–18`). The latter is about 3×10^−5 nat per trial at D=4 for one score, before allowing errors in both compared scores. Such checked errors are small relative to the declared effects; they do not explain the enormous scale-tail points. They do not bound every optimizer visit, changed optimum/selection, or the most extreme datasets. Check extreme concepts and both winning densities at their actual cross-fitted parameters before attributing every tail observation solely to statistics. `paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:29` correctly states this limited assurance. Older GH failures and stale comments are not evidence that the production trapezoid calculation currently fails.

- **The simulation omits important dependence in intended data.** Labels called families have no family-specific generating distribution here; independently drawn concept effects make the eight strata statistically interchangeable apart from their labels (`simulate.py:138–155`). Real family distributions, carrier effects, shared/nested packets and clause reuse are not simulated. The likelihood also assumes conditional trial independence given its concept effect. Preserving a concept block in resampling does not establish that this likelihood models those dependencies. These are application assumptions, not measured causes of this synthetic failure. The finite eligible concept bank also requires declaring whether inference targets that bank, conditional on the frozen CAL instrument and families, or a broader concept population (`paper:sections/appendix.tex:13–18`).

## Q3 — Repair ranking and the eight-group proposal

**Verdict: F1 is the first low-cost correction; a correctly stratified full-refit jackknife is the most useful cheaper F2 candidate for milder settings; F3 is a high-priority predictor-design repair for scale variation. Whole-family jackknife plus `t_7` is not sound for the stated target. No combination is validated yet.**

For the existing twelve-setting target, rank immediate work as follows:

1. **F1, essentially no fitting cost.** Use the exact within-stratum variance calculation for a normal/studentized candidate, or the declared affine percentile correction as a candidate. It addresses all twelve settings' fixed-score finite-stratum variance convention, lifts the two marginal failures in development, and leaves the six original passes intact. It does not fix the four residual failures. Do not multiply a jackknife variance by an additional F1 factor if that jackknife already estimates the unbiased stratified variance.

2. **F2 redesigned, first on `tau=0.5`, `alpha=1`, `omega=0.5` and a homogeneous control.** Preserve every family in every deletion replicate and refit the complete selection/scoring algorithm. This can capture cross-concept influence that fixed-score variance omits. It is the strongest affordable candidate for the residual milder failures; alpha 1 is worth retaining even though F1 narrowly crosses its floor. Expected benefit at omega 1 is uncertain and omega 2 is a poor first variance purchase. Validate all retained settings after choosing a method; passing the original six does not certify a changed algorithm.

3. **F3, best directed at the scale-tail mechanism and scale-heterogeneous two-state detection.** A mixture member integrating a concept log-scale is scientifically motivated. It can give both families a way to predict an atypical scale, rather than forcing that variation into component means/global scales. It should be assessed on graded scale generators and on *new two-state scale generators*. It changes the contrast, selected members, gain references and potential false calls throughout all twelve settings; improvement everywhere is not guaranteed. A separate M3S does not automatically handle simultaneous random thresholds and scales, correlated effects, skew or non-Gaussian tails. Nor must every model comparison be symmetric in every parameter to be valid. F3 is a substantive expansion to test, not a mathematical prerequisite for any valid interval.

4. **Full pipeline bootstrap, a more expensive comparator rather than the sole remaining solution.** It represents fitting/selection variability but has the support and nonregularity issues above. It costs 80 or 200 nested pipeline fits per interval. Keep it as development evidence and a comparator if affordable; the same resampling scheme alone does not prove that multiplying it by 1.069 corrects its full variance.

The two legitimate jackknife constructions are different:

- A **stratified delete-one-concept** jackknife uses all 64 perturbations, with original stratum weights preserved. For a fixed-score mean its variance is `sum_h [(n_h−1)/n_h] sum_i (T_(h,−i)−mean_i T_(h,−i))²`, which recovers `sum_h W_h² s_h²/n_h`. The refitted version is a natural variance candidate under an approximately smooth/stable procedure. Simply averaging the remaining 63 concepts without correcting the changed stratum weight is not that construction.
- A **balanced delete-group** approximation uses eight groups, each formed by independently randomizing within each family and taking one concept from every family. Delete one such group, leaving seven concepts in each family; score with the original family weights. For balanced groups the usual candidate is `V_J=(7/8) sum_g (T_(−g)−mean_g T_(−g))²`. The grouping should be frozen independently of losses. The grouping principle follows stratified replicate-weight practice; applying it to this non-smooth CV estimator still needs validation. [Kott, original NASS report, stratified sampling and Appendix A](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Education_and_Outreach/Reports,_Presentations_and_Conferences/allreports/Using_the_Delete-a-Group_Jackknife_Estimator_in_NASS_Surveys.pdf).

Use common declared fold/start streams for paired perturbations when estimating data influence, and separately assess partition/algorithm randomness if the target averages over it. Arbitrary reindexing that reshuffles every fold after a deletion can overwhelm the influence being estimated. This mapping and the treatment of smaller training sets must be part of the candidate definition, not left to defaults.

With eight groups, `t_(.975,7)=2.364624`, 20.6% larger than 1.96. That is a reasonable cautious *candidate* critical value under nearly Gaussian group influences, not an exact distributional result. There are only seven variance degrees of freedom; in the ideal normal approximation, variance estimates have relative SD about `sqrt(2/7)=0.535`. Heavy tails and selection discontinuities can be worse. More balanced random groupings can assess grouping sensitivity; they reuse the same 64 concepts and do not create independent sampling units.

Nominal fitting-count comparisons, before measured runtime differences: eight deletions versus 200 bootstrap fits is 25× fewer; eight versus 80 is 10× fewer; 64 versus 200 is 3.125× fewer. Deletion fits use fewer concepts, but optimizer difficulty means these are not measured time ratios. At the paper's 23.6-core-hour refit proxy, eight full pipelines would be about 189 core-hours per dataset versus 4,720 for 200; do not treat either extrapolation as a new benchmark. Adding one retained member adds 25 member-fits per layer (five outer folds × four inner fits plus outer refit), a 12.5% increase in fit count over eight members; a hierarchical M3S may cost much more per fit. Its actual runtime and numerical integration need measurement.

Two other designs deserve consideration:

- **A direct calibrated test on the point statistic**, already mentioned in `paper:sections/methods.tex:125`. If the scientific deliverable is generating-family discrimination, calibrating a critical value against a sufficiently broad composite graded null avoids estimating a highly unstable mean merely to make a decision. This needs independent null/alternative calibration, near-boundary and misspecified generators, unavailable-case rules and finite-Monte-Carlo guarantees. It changes the inferential claim and does not repair an interval for theta. Its evaluation needs original pipeline fits but no 80–200 refits per dataset.
- **A different target or predictive tail model.** A predeclared bounded contrast, or genuinely heavy-tailed predictive distributions in both families, could reduce rare-concept leverage. Clipping the score abandons the original unmodified log-score contrast and its direct propriety interpretation; trimming targets the expectation of a different finite-sample statistic. A frozen-training/independent-test design would simplify conditional risk inference but would also change the target and reduce training/testing information. These are follow-on designs, not deadline substitutes silently inserted into this paper.

## Q5 — What else is failing?

**Verdict: several instrument components remain inadequate for confirmation, but they do not invalidate the paper's three bounded descriptive claims. The most serious failures for the held study are interval/reference validity, fitting robustness, and generalization to the intended stimulus/readout design. The matched human extension has independent failed gates.**

Here C1 is partial human reproduction, C2 is the declared synthetic call-count result, C3 is failure of the original interval criterion, and H is the held model study. Ranking below is by inferential consequence, not novelty. Counts quoted from the matched-comparison paper are reported evidence, not new computations of that separate battery.

### (a) Measured failures or demonstrated inferential defects

1. **Primary uncertainty fails its own minimum inclusion criterion.** Six observed failures, four still below after F1; the largest shortfalls are unambiguous as empirical failures, though true coverage is unknown. Fatal for H's current confirmation rule; supports C3 rather than threatening it. Evidence: reproduced rates above; `paper:sections/results.tex:71,210–234`.

2. **The omega-2 reference is practically unstable.** Independent-bank mean/MCSE −6.87/2.68, one deletion moves the mean 38.5%; the original bank has the same qualitative problem. A feasible validated interval for this target is not supplied. Fatal for a claimed coverage validation at that setting; appropriately qualifies C3. Evidence: reproduced bank above; `paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:40–52`.

3. **Fitting can miss much better optima while recording convergence.** The saved development audit reports 161/1,536 member-fits with a training gap >0.5 nat; maximum gap 8,652.182 nat, including outer M3V under omega 2. Its outer-size family-comparison proxy changed by >0.001 nat/trial in 10/96 units, with zero sign changes. That proxy is explicitly not a full rerun of nested selection, and a stronger search is not proof of a global optimum. This threatens stability of H and interpretation of numerical mechanisms, but does not erase C2's historical procedure-specific counts. Evidence: `reviews/pc_audit_2026-09-14/results/audit_fits.md:7–39,180–190`; `models.py:650–705`. The paper's “every retained fit met the recorded convergence criterion” is compatible with this failure, not a contradiction (`paper:sections/results.tex:67`).

4. **The matched human comparison does not pass its calibration/completion gates.** O5 gives 12/100 two-state calls versus at most one allowed; W4 has four unavailable sets, counted against that limit; all 50 T2 sets are unavailable; T3 is absent after a failed reference search. O5's generating family is unknown, so 12/100 is a *failed robustness gate*, not a measured false-positive rate under known graded truth. High-latency profile coverage is 0.848/0.802 in the graded family and 0.792/0.726 in the two-state family at 40/80 ms. These findings prevent the extension from deciding whether the human preference survives latency/scale variation or from identifying its latency mechanism. They do not refute C1's claim of partial reproduction. Evidence: `paper:sections/jitter_methods.tex:15`; `jitter_results.tex:10–28`; `jitter_discussion.tex:6,25`.

5. **Human numerical reproduction is partial and fitting-sensitive.** Published/ported pxp curves have r=0.88, not equality; the first sustained ranking run differs. A <0.2%-of-spread decoder change moves the first mixture ranking from 315 to 285 ms and late graded return from 675 to 735 ms. Most likelihood fits hit their original evaluation cap, and about 30% of restarted two-state fits still hit the larger cap. This prevents a precise estimator-independent onset claim; C1 already states the correct narrower result. Evidence: `paper:sections/results.tex:35–55`.

6. **The inherited pair gives wrong generating-family verdicts on declared graded data.** There are 989 false mixture calls: 651 at tau 2, 337 at omega 2, one at omega 1. This demonstrates predictor misspecification plus its associated comparison procedure, not a general type-I error of the human test or an isolated effect of one added member. It supports C2's transfer caution. Evidence: `paper:sections/results.tex:75,93,153–166`; saved decisions in `reproduced.json`.

7. **The inherited human graphical rule has no calibrated frequentist interpretation.** Predictive scores supplied to BMS are not marginal evidences, and `1−pxp` is not a null p-value; the Simes/BH operation therefore supplies no demonstrated error control. Removing the distant null changes pairwise pxp markedly. These are demonstrated interpretation defects, not an empirical estimate of the original procedure's false-call rate. C1 remains a reproduction of a descriptive convention. Evidence: `paper:sections/methods.tex:38–50`; `results.tex:49–51`; `supplement_audit.tex:17`.

8. **Several alternatives tried during development failed to provide the missing assurance.** Basic, BCa, studentized and normal fixed-score variants did not pass all twelve settings; the new F1 screen also fails four. The matched human fitter's initial shared covariance produced two-state calls in 6/19 known graded development sets, and missed optima, runaway template coefficients and an early-stopped EM loop were repaired. These are historical development failures, not defects that should be asserted to persist in the repaired code. Evidence: `paper:sections/methods.tex:127`; `jitter_results.tex:6–8`; new calculations above.

The dip/BIC/steepness results concern *other summaries*. Dip misses most declared mixtures and BIC selects mixtures in many declared graded datasets, but their targets differ. They are not further failures of this conditional predictive-family procedure and do not justify a general ranking of those methods (`paper:sections/results.tex:97–102`).

### (b) Untested assumptions and missing validations, ranked

1. **No replacement interval, changed start policy or expanded predictor family has passed independent validation of the complete procedure.** A non-stop at the current screen cannot supply that. Refitting support/selection issues, precise outer-fold conditioning and uncertain reference estimation remain. F3 additionally requires predictor/numerical audit, new gains, null calibration and sensitivity. Evidence: `paper:sections/methods.tex:124–129`; Q2–Q4.

2. **Synthetic truth is narrow and far from the decision boundary.** All twelve graded generators are retained members, all original intervals are below zero, and the largest upper endpoint is −0.0067. Neither near-zero error control nor a broad misspecified graded population was tested. The 599/600 mixture detections are not size-controlled power. The alternatives lack concept scale heterogeneity, weaker effects and combined nuisance structure; the formal full power/recovery grid has not run. This is the largest generalization gap behind C2 if its wording were broadened, and a major H risk. Evidence: `paper:sections/results.tex:95,201–205`; `methods.tex:117`.

3. **The actual model-side design is not the validated synthetic design.** Revised three-family bank versus eight identical synthetic strata; finite eligible bank and acceptance checks; CAL/PILOT/CONF split; D and dose placement; full multi-layer band and workspace-minus-early contrast; target R2 and secondary readouts/conditions. The one-layer, D=4, C=64 results do not certify these. The 16-concept development pilot is particularly not a sensitivity test at the confirmatory design. Evidence: `paper:sections/methods.tex:93,122`; `results.tex:263`; `appendix.tex:11–18,28,34`.

4. **The projection and stimulus channel have not been tested end to end for manufactured or destroyed two-state structure.** The audit simulates scalar readouts, not activations followed by endpoint-trained decoders. A decoder can emphasize or suppress structure; clause count is not equal semantic evidence; carriers, categories, backgrounds, repeated clauses and packet nesting can introduce dependencies or mixtures. Decoder technical accuracy alone does not validate the construct. Specify the population and whether inference conditions on a frozen CAL decoder before deciding which upstream randomness must be propagated. Evidence: `paper:sections/discussion.tex:37`; `appendix.tex:13–18,34`; `simulate.py:138–155`.

5. **The bridge from predictive preference to functional access and causal state use is untested.** R1-to-R2 prediction at fixed dose, state assignments, enough high/low trials for matching, causal swaps and controls, and advantage over a smooth moderation account have no confirmatory results. A mixture label is not established ontology. These are essential H conditions, not conclusions implied by a repaired interval. Evidence: `paper:sections/appendix.tex:20–28,34`.

6. **Human inferential robustness beyond reproduction remains incomplete.** Leakage from all-trial starting values, non-nested decoder/likelihood CV and non-block decoder folds is inherited and unquantified; block 21 is always training in four datasets; decibel labels are uncertain in four datasets. The passive comparison has no inherited-family recovery study or paired active/passive advantage analysis, uses separate decoders, and the active reference was selected after seeing outcomes. One dataset, auditory protocol and decoder do not establish wider physiology. Evidence: `paper:sections/methods.tex:21,31–36,50`; `discussion.tex:37`; `results.tex:55`; `supplement_audit.tex:19–22`.

7. **Numerical assurance is local, and model-based latent quantities need separate targets.** Quadrature has not been bounded everywhere; a richer fitter can change the statistic rather than merely its SE. Information decompositions conditional on a fitted latent label do not establish that label, and their dose prior, concept-effect handling and uncertainty are still to be frozen. One quantized checkpoint and correlational readouts do not establish cross-system or mechanistic equivalence. Evidence: `paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:29`; `theory.tex:57,78`; `discussion.tex:27–29,37`.

### (c) Things that look fine within their stated scope

- The score difference, own-replicate reference, family-block order, and the twelve original inclusion rates reproduce. The concept is a sensible block for a joint concept likelihood. Exchangeable concept *inputs* do not imply independent out-of-fold scores, but this does not make trials a better bootstrap unit (`analyze.py:251,312–338`; `paper:sections/results.tex:214`).
- Outer test concepts are excluded from fitting and inner member selection. Bootstrap copies remain together at both levels, avoiding duplicate-concept train/test leakage (`analyze.py:114–136,199–251,668–674`). This is valuable even though full-bootstrap coverage remains unproven.
- The log-score comparison is coherent for declared predictive densities; its nats-per-trial unit is common without implying equal effects across substrates. The procedure-level estimand is explicitly distinguished from gain and the risk of one frozen fit (`paper:sections/theory.tex:42–67`; `transfer.tex:41–45`).
- All 12,000 primary/ensemble graded calls and the stated 599/600 detection result remain legitimate historical, setting-specific outcomes. Zero in 1,000 has one-sided 95% upper bound 0.002991 per setting, not a simultaneous or universal guarantee (`paper:sections/methods.tex:111–115`; `results.tex:69,184–205`).
- Capture parity, held-out endpoint decoder performance and the pilot adapter establish useful technical operation. They do not establish access or interval validity, and the current text says so (`paper:sections/supplement_audit.tex:69–71`; `results.tex:240–263`).
- The held status, independent-seed requirement, explicit failure denominators and the screen's failure-only language are sound safeguards. The matched comparison appropriately reports its real contrast descriptively after failed gates. Preserve these boundaries (`PREREGISTRATION_T1_model.md:862–874`; `paper:sections/jitter_results.tex:28`).
