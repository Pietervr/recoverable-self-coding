## 1. The target problem and why I would not buy a new ordinary bank first

The intended mean target remains the expectation of the complete fitted/selected procedure under its declared validity convention. It is not a population median, a trimmed mean, the screen's own average, or an oracle gain. Where original fits fail, the conditional valid-point target must be distinguished from an unconditional expectation, as S3 already states.

S4's historical, independent 1,000-dataset omega-2 bank gave mean −6.87, MCSE 2.68, median −2.07 and one dataset at −2,650. Removing that dataset moved the mean to −4.22. These are old-policy results; the amended start policy can change both expectation and dispersion. Their diagnostic ±2 MCSE range is not a new-policy confidence set. Neither the median nor deleting extremes repairs the expectation target.

The current `row22_target.py` is an **omega-1** evaluator: mean ± Student-t critical value × MCSE for a two-sided 99% interval, complete compatible independent records, failure/tail/precision/even-odd gates, and a 0.10-nat half-width criterion. Its trimmed-sample check is a diagnostic, not a bound on unseen tail contribution. Those checks do not manufacture 99% coverage for arbitrary heavy-tailed statistics. The 0.10 criterion was sized for omega 1; it is not an automatically necessary omega-2 tolerance, nor may a new tolerance be chosen after seeing which value makes screen coverage look acceptable.

The old SD proxy is `2.68×sqrt(1000)≈84.75`. **For scale only**, if that dispersion persisted, a nominal 99% mean interval with half-width 0.10 would require about **4.77 million originals**, roughly **127 million core-hours** at 26.6 hours per original. Even spending all 100,000 hours on about 3,759 originals would give an illustrative half-width around 3.56. A 408-bank's proxy half-width is about 10.86. These extrapolations are not forecasts of the amended policy or proofs that no better estimator can work. They show why an affordable new bank cannot be promised to resolve the target from present evidence.

The code supplies no useful certified score bound, variance upper bound or tail remainder. Held-out log scores are not clipped (`analyze.py:247–260,446–455`); Gaussian log density is unbounded in residuals/scales (`models.py:230–264`), and the optimizer has no general parameter bounds (`:640–648`). The `1e30` objective fallback handles nonfinite training evaluations; it is not a cap on valid held-out statistics. Finite lognormal generator moments do not prove finite variance of the complete fitted/selected statistic; S4 explicitly preserves that distinction. This is uncertainty about the complete statistic, not a finding that its mean or variance is infinite.

A conventional mean-coverage screen would need a **new, independent, policy-matched reference strategy with justified uncertainty** before its coverage release. A 384-original bank at 24 cores/48 h needs 16 jobs, **18,432 core-hours of reservation**, and could still be UNRESOLVED. A 1,000-original bank needs 42 full jobs, **48,384 hours**. Adding even the 384-bank to the recommended reservation budget gives **117,120 hours**, beyond HEAL's 100,000. Smaller bank-first/refit designs can fit, but no record establishes that their bank would yield an interpretable mean target.

Therefore **do not freeze a purportedly RESOLVED finite numerical T here, and do not spend this package on a new ordinary mean bank**. A same-expectation robust mean estimator or importance-sampling/tail-stratified estimator could be developed later. Robust mean estimation is not inherently an estimand change, but its confidence radius needs justified moment conditions/constants; [Devroye et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05845) make that setting explicit. Such a strategy has not been implemented or calibrated for this complete procedure. A bounded-score revision would instead change the procedure/target and need fresh calibration.

## 2. The recommended alternative: a bank-free universal-futility screen

Freeze the **search domain of all finite real constants**, denoted T = R, in a new explicit analysis contract. This is not an estimated 99% reference interval and makes no claim to identify the expectation. It contains every finite candidate target deterministically, so there is no bank noncoverage error to allocate.

For usable closed intervals `I_i`, define `K(theta)=sum_i 1{theta in I_i}` and `Ksup=max(theta in R) K(theta)`, the maximum number overlapping at any common constant. Test the composite null:

`H0: some fixed finite theta has P(theta in I) >= 0.90`.

Use a **single one-sided 97.5% Clopper–Pearson upper bound**. Declare **UNIVERSAL FUTILITY** only if `U_CP(Ksup,n)<0.90`. For any fixed witness `theta0` satisfying H0, `Ksup>=K(theta0)`, so

`P{U_CP(Ksup,n)<.90} <= P{U_CP(K(theta0),n)<.90} <= .025`.

The last step is exact binomial-tail inversion ([NIST's exact binomial bounds](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/exacbino.htm)). Maximizing the count makes rejection harder; it needs no search multiplicity penalty. This is a necessary-condition test, not selection of a favorable low-inclusion target. With **24 usable intervals, reject at Ksup ≤17**: the upper bound is 0.873848 at 17/24 and 0.902270 at 18/24.

The argument assumes independent datasets and the same fixed interval/validity procedure for each. Allocation, retries and the sealed look cannot depend on observed interval outcomes. Resource qualification may inspect technical validity and timing, not make interim coverage decisions.

The error claim is **2.5% for this one primary stress decision**, with no additional 1% bank term. The adopted omega-1 decision has its separate target/screen error accounting. The two setting-level decisions do not jointly promise 2.5% familywise error; a union bound is at most 5%, subject to omega 1's target assumptions. No formal decision is added for every comparison variant or band: selection/ws is primary, paired alternatives remain secondary diagnostics. Do not add looks after seeing results.

Missingness follows the adopted conservative convention. Let n be the number usable and M = 24−n. Require **both** the CP upper bound at `(Ksup,n)` and the all-attempt bound at `(Ksup+M,24)` to fall below 0.90. The latter requires **Ksup+M ≤17**. When n=0, report unavailable and make no conditional test. This grants missing intervals the most favorable possible inclusion for rejection, without assuming missing-at-random or pretending a missing result is a scientific zero. Report original failures, per-variant refit failures/reasons and paired availability separately. Nonconvergence-related missingness deserves explicit reporting, as [Morris, White and Crowther](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086) discuss.

Compute Ksup by a pure sorted-endpoint sweep. At a shared coordinate, add starts, count overlap, then remove ends, so touching intervals count inclusively. Outside all finite intervals the count is zero. No arbitrary grid, sample-mean target, interval midpoint guess or ±infinity arithmetic is needed.

**Power is limited and may be zero for a failure at the true expectation.** If the intervals consistently cover a different central constant, the universal test may never reject even though coverage of theta_g is poor. Known-singleton-target binomial rejection probabilities at n=24/cutoff 17 are 90.4% at coverage .60, 61.1% at .70, 39.3% at .75, 18.9% at .80 and 5.72% at .85. These are **upper bounds**, not power forecasts for the maximum-overlap test. Its exact boundary error at a singleton p=.90 is 0.7456%. There is no useful universal lower-power guarantee based only on coverage of theta_g.

There is **no positive rule** here. The minimum overlap over all real constants is zero outside the union of finitely many finite intervals. More fundamentally, non-rejection neither estimates theta_g nor validates its coverage. Outcomes are **universal futility / not ruled out at this look / unavailable**, never PASS. If an interpretable numerical expectation-coverage estimate is required, that remains a different, unresolved target-estimation task.

## 4. Crash exposure and the default resource policy

**A numerical omega-2 increase in LLVM ENOMEM/SIGSEGV rate cannot be estimated from these records.** The score tail is severe, but omega 2 changes values/scales, not the declared numbers of concepts, trials, models or padded graph dimensions. Optimizer behavior and runtime may change. Heavy statistical tails do not by themselves establish greater compiler RAM use. Existing omega-1 repeated crashes and Q2's crash are not a matched omega-2 packing cohort.

Use the ordinary intended 24-worker geometry only after the prospective omega-2 pilot qualifies. Do **not** make a 256 GiB/fat node the blanket default without evidence; its core allocation/billing and numerical behavior are not free or established. The successful 2.32 GiB worker RSS is not a bound on compilation transients, and the current 4.5 GiB guard checks at resample boundaries, not inside a fit.

Keep the adopted rule: after **two same-envelope fatal allocation/signal attempts**, no third identical attempt. A remaining coordinate may have one reviewed rescue with lower packing/more measured headroom, preserving its dataset, seed, folds, draw, starts and scientific identity. Every reattempt, rescue pilot or additional qualification is charged to the 4,608-hour pool. A 48-core/48 h request consumes 2,304 hours; the pool buys two such jobs, **not four**. An actually different charged node shape must be priced before admission. Twelve useful workers on a billed 24-core node can roughly double core-hours per finished refit and does not preserve this budget automatically.

If ordinary packing or the rescue cannot qualify/finish within the bounded resources, stop progression and report the technical outcome. Keep the coordinate missing; never exclude it as harmless, substitute a new resample, invent a failed-fit record for an absent process output, or adjust optimizer starts to force completion. No current omega-1 job is stopped or changed by this policy proposal.

## 6. What the papers and protocol freeze may claim

Report the two settings separately with their different decision contracts. Omega 1 tests an adopted finite estimated target set; omega 2's proposed test searches all finite constants without estimating the target. Do not pool their counts, use omega 1's numerical bank at omega 2, or label them two validated settings.

If omega 2 rejects, the SI may state, with the exact method/level and usable/attempted denominators, that the finite-B replacement failed a **reference-free necessary condition for 0.90 coverage at this stress setting**: the screen rejects the hypothesis that some common finite constant has coverage at least 0.90. The interpretation as failure of mean coverage is conditional on a finite theta_g existing. It cannot give a new expectation value, claim to have repaired the historical reference, identify the tail mechanism as the cause of a coverage result, or validate another interval.

If it does not reject, report **universal futility not established; expectation coverage remains unresolved**. Empirical maximum overlap is not a coverage estimate at theta_g. Even if both screens survive, there is no PASS and no protocol-freeze go.

A validation that supports freeze still requires a fixed usable procedure and estimand; a justified independent target/reference strategy with uncertainty; prespecified positive success criteria and adequate precision/power; complete failure/availability accounting; independent datasets not used to choose the method; and the retained generators, design sizes and model-band rule required by the intended confirmation. Single-layer omega 1 plus this stress diagnostic do not establish those broader requirements. If either rejects, change and independently revalidate the procedure before confirmation. If both are inconclusive, the target/positive-validation work remains, rather than being waived by exhausted budgets.
