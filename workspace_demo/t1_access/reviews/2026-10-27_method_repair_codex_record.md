`q_M3S,c = integral product_i [(1-A(k_i)) N(y_i; mu_low, sigma^2 exp(2v)) + A(k_i) N(y_i; mu_high(k_i), sigma^2 exp(2v))] N(v;0,omega^2) dv`.

For concept `c`, calculate `d_c=(log q_X,c-log q_G,c)/n_c`, then `z_c=clip(d_c,-a,a)`, with **a=0.03**. With fixed family weights `W_h=n_h/64`, the statistic is

`T = sum_h W_h mean_{c in h}(z_c)`.

At the proposed role allocation this is the ordinary mean of the bounded concept contrasts. The target is **theta_g = median_g(T)** across repeated complete studies at this design, with the declared bank/packet sampling and fitting algorithm; formally choose the lower median `inf{t: P_g(T<=t)>=1/2}`. This changed target answers: *in a typical repetition of this study, is the concept-balanced bounded predictive contrast positive?* It gives each held-out concept a limited score contribution. Holding the fitted predictors fixed, replacing one concept score changes `T` by at most `2a/64=0.0009375`; refitting after changing a concept can affect all scores, so that bound is **not** a stability theorem for the entire fitting algorithm. The entire statistic lies in `[-0.03,0.03]`. Full-pipeline calibration below handles shared fitting variability without assuming independent out-of-fold losses. Source: the declared statistic in `repair_procedure.py` and the arithmetic above.

The density ensembles are proper predictive densities. **Clipping their log-score difference is not a proper scoring rule.** This is the smallest redesign I recommend accepting: retain the uncapped log score as a descriptive sensitivity result and change the primary estimand openly. A positive result is bounded predictive preference relative to a specified graded challenge set; it does not establish a biological or computational state ontology. ### Interval, critical value, and fitting uncertainty

Use a frozen catalogue `G` of generators, including graded and mixture settings, at the final intended design. For each setting generate an independent calibration bank of complete datasets. **Every replicate repeats training fits and held-out scoring.** There is no bootstrap inside an observed-data interval, no reuse of one dataset's fitted scores as if they were independent observations, and no selected winner. Fitting variance, fold overlap, random concept effects and any prescribed optimizer randomness enter through the distribution of the entire pipeline statistic. The guarantee is for catalogue distributions generated by this complete algorithm; a finite catalogue does not certify its continuum or arbitrary data-generating processes.

The following construction is implemented in `scratch:repair_procedure.py:median_bracket,upper_tolerance,calibrate,apply`:

1. For each setting form an exact binomial order-statistic bracket `[m_g^-,m_g^+]` for its distribution median. Allocate total error probability 0.005 across these brackets.
2. Form lower and upper tolerance limits `[l_g,u_g]` for the distribution of `T`, covering at least its central 0.96 probability. Allocate total calibration failure probability 0.005 across both tails and all settings. For an upper limit at content `p=0.98`, choose the smallest one-based rank `k` satisfying `BinomCDF(k-1;R,p) >= 1-delta`, and take the `k`th ordered calibration statistic. Apply the same construction to `-T` for the lower limit. Ties are conservative.
3. Freeze `A=max_g(u_g-m_g^-,0)` and `B=max_g(m_g^+-l_g,0)`. Report `I(T)=[max(-a,T-A), min(a,T+B)]`.
4. Freeze the graded-null critical value `c_G=max(0,max_{g graded} u_g)`. Report **mixture preference** only if the interval's lower endpoint is positive **and** `T>c_G`. A negative upper endpoint is a **negative bounded contrast**, not proof that the generator is graded. Otherwise report inconclusive. A failed fit or unavailable score is unavailable, never a scientific decision.

Why this works within the catalogue: on the common calibration event, whose probability is at least 0.99, every median is bracketed and `P_g(l_g<=T<=u_g)>=0.96`. For such a `T`, `T-A<=theta_g<=T+B`, by the definitions of `A` and `B`. Thus conditional inclusion is at least 0.96, and unconditional inclusion over calibration and a new study is at least `0.99*0.96=0.9504`. The extra graded critical value bounds a structural graded challenge even when its predictive contrast is positive. On the same event its positive-call probability is at most 0.02, and unconditionally at most `0.99*0.02+0.01=0.0298`. These are elementary order-statistic/union-bound results, not a bootstrap asymptotic assertion. They assume independent, finite complete pipeline statistics and an unchanged catalogue and algorithm. Numerical unavailability is additionally controlled through attempted-set validation, rather than silently conditioning its denominator away. Derivation and constants: `repair_procedure.py`; calibration ranks: `repair_costs.json`.

For the proposed calibration size and catalogue, the median bracket uses ranks 208 and 293, and the tail tolerance limits use the minimum and maximum of the 500 calibration points. This is deliberately conservative. The independent validation, not a same-bank calculation, decides whether the frozen artifact has useful coverage, availability and sensitivity. Source: `repair_costs.json`.

### Disposition of the other candidate repairs

- **Finite-stratum correction:** keep it for fixed-score descriptive uncertainty. For `s_h^2` with denominator `n_h-1`, the unbiased independent-score variance reference is `sum W_h^2 s_h^2/n_h`; the empirical within-stratum bootstrap instead has factors `(n_h-1)/n_h`. Inflate each stratum's *centered contribution* by `sqrt(n_h/(n_h-1))` before combining. With equal strata of size eight this reduces to endpoint deviations multiplied by `sqrt(8/7)`. With unequal strata there is no one universal factor. It is exact for the linear fixed-score variance comparison, not for all refitted estimators. The chosen primary uses no such bootstrap and consequently needs no hidden Bessel multiplier. Sources: `analyze.py:312–338`; round-1 record Q1; `scratch:reproduced.json`.
- **Refit bootstrap:** not primary. It includes fitting but still samples only observed concept support and perturbs the distinct-concept training size. Increasing its replicate count does not add unseen scale extremes. Its present cost precludes whole-grid qualification; see S3. Sources: `analyze.py:613–674`; round-1 `reproduced.json` resampling calculation.
- **Concept/deletion-group jackknife with refit:** a reasonable variance diagnostic after changing the target, not an automatic interval guarantee. Delete individual concepts within strata, retaining the fixed target weights; a within-stratum variance form is `sum_h (n_h-1)/n_h * sum_c (T_-hc - mean_c T_-hc)^2`. It propagates fitting for a sufficiently regular statistic but does not establish validity for hard selection or unseen tails. Balanced delete-groups can reduce cost, but groups must represent all strata in the intended proportions. **Do not delete whole families**: these are fixed strata, and three family deletions do not create an adequate sampling distribution for this concept target. Costs are extra complete point fits per deletion/group, rather than free calculations from saved scores. Sources: fixed strata `simulate.py:81–89`; round-1 record findings 4–5 and Q3.
- **Direct simulation critical values:** adopted, with complete-pipeline calibration and a separate positive-contrast interval. This is the paper's option of calibrating the statistic directly, made executable rather than buying refits within every interval. It avoids the small-stratum bootstrap and its reduced empirical support. It remains a model-based certification against a declared challenge set.
- **Student-t residual members:** worthwhile model development if uncapped log scoring must remain the scientific target. A t density changes tail penalties; its log score is still unbounded and does not alone account for fitting covariance. Censored log scores and bounded proper kernel scores require new predictive probabilities or samples. They are not recoverable by relabelling stored Gaussian likelihood values. They require new fits/score calibration and are more extensive changes than the chosen bounded contrast.
- **Trimmed or per-concept median targets:** checked in round 1. Their fixed-score intervals did not solve the failures. The primary here instead uses the median of the **complete study statistic** and calibrates its full pipeline distribution. These are different operations. Source: round-1 `reproduced.json`, `robust` fields and Q1.
- **M3S:** adopted in the proposed point algorithm to address scale asymmetry and the missing heterogeneous-scale alternative. Its local comparison is development evidence (S2); fresh nulls and alternatives must be simulated with it. Adding this member does not validate a pre-existing interval or preserve a pre-existing power claim.
