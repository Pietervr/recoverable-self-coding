# Reproduction of Sergent et al. 2021 (Nat Commun 12:1149) — single-trial model comparison

Owning document for the port in this folder (pipeline and every deviation: `README.md`).
Run 2026-09-11 by a job agent for the `Entropy` session (RSC Entropy special-issue paper, primary
study T1, human-EEG half). Data: OSF `aw3t5`, the 20 open subjects. **This is a reproduction of the
authors' own MATLAB/MNE pipeline on their own data, not an independent re-analysis** — where the
prose and the code differ, the code was followed (README D-list).

## What was run

| step | active session | passive session |
|---|---|---|
| single-trial decoder (63 ch, 500 Hz, 1250 samples, 10-fold, no-sound vs max level) | 20/20 subjects, 18,054 trials | 20/20 subjects, 19,526 trials (S11–S20 with the 7th level, −3 dB) |
| model fits: 53 windows × 3 models × 5 block-folds = 795 Nelder–Mead runs per subject | 20/20 (15,900 fits) | 20/20 (15,900 fits) |
| group Bayesian model selection (spm_BMS port, 10⁶ Dirichlet samples per window), Simes correction | 53 windows | 53 windows |
| mean/SD-by-SNR profiles, rmANOVAs, neuro-behavioural correlations | 8 article windows + every sample | 8 windows + every sample (no behaviour in the passive session) |
| sensitivity run: decoder with `lbfgs` instead of `liblinear` (README D2), full fits + BMS | 20/20 | — |
| sensitivity run: decoder trained on level 6 with the −3 dB trials excluded for S11–S20 (README D14), full fits + BMS | — | 20/20 |

Wall time on the Mac (≤ 6 cores): decoding ≈ 12 s per dataset, fitting ≈ 20 s per dataset, BMS ≈ 1 min per session.
All numbers below are in `results/*.csv` and `figures/*.csv`; the PNGs are regenerated by
`make_figures.py` / `model_comparison.py` (they are gitignored).

### C6. Bayesian model comparison, active session (paper Fig. 3E: "During baseline, the pxp of the Null model was slightly above the other two. Then a first period between 0 and 250 ms where the unimodal model showed the highest probability. After 250 ms, a switch in favor of the bifurcation model, whose pxp remained above 95 % for most of the period between 250 and 700 ms. Then a last period between 700 and 900 ms where the unimodal model seemed most favored again.")

Protected exceedance probabilities per 30 ms window, 20 subjects (`results/bms_active.csv`,
`figures/fig_b_model_comparison_active.csv/.png`; window centres in ms):

| period | paper | port: winner and pxp | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| baseline −285 … +15 | null slightly above | **null** in all 11 windows, pxp 0.38–0.66 (2B and 3 ≈ 0.17–0.32) | reproduced |
| 45 … 285 | unimodal highest | 2B at 75–165 (0.36 → 0.93), 225 (0.995), 285 (0.77); bifurcation at 195 (0.82); tie at 255 (0.50/0.50); null ≈ 0 from 165 on | reproduced (with a blip at 195 ms) |
| switch | after 250 ms | between 285 (2B 0.77) and **315 ms (bifurcation 0.96)** | reproduced, ≈ 50 ms later |
| bifurcation period | > 0.95 for most of 250–700 | **> 0.95 in 10 of the 12 windows from 315 to 645 ms**: 315 0.96, 345 0.995, 375 0.996, 405 0.98, 435–495 0.999, 525 0.996, 555 0.99, 585 0.86, 615 0.99, 645 0.94; Simes-corrected significant at 345, 375, 435, 465, 495, 525 ms | reproduced (300–660 instead of 250–700 ms) |
| late unimodal period | 700–900 | **2B at 675–825 ms**, pxp 0.81–0.98 | reproduced |
| after 850 | not described | indeterminate (bifurcation 0.65–0.92 at 855–885, then all ≈ 1/3 with null back on top from ≈ 1200 ms) | — |

What the group evidence is made of at the peak (435–495 ms): the bifurcation model has the higher
cross-validated log-likelihood in 12–14 of the 20 subjects, its Dirichlet expected frequency is
0.84–0.85, and the mean per-subject advantage over the unimodal model is small but consistent —
ΔLLH(3 − 2B) = 0.43–0.53 nats ± 0.16–0.20 SEM (t ≈ 2.5–3.0), against ΔLLH(2B − 0) ≈ 13–18 nats for the
SNR effect itself. The Bayes omnibus risk is ≤ 0.024 from 195 to 795 ms (≤ 0.01 at 315–555 ms).

**Verdict: reproduced.** All four periods of Fig. 3E are present in the same order with the same
winners; the one quantitative difference is that the bifurcation period starts at 315 ms here
(pxp 0.96) rather than "after 250 ms" — at 255 and 285 ms the two models are tied or unimodal-
leaning — and ends at ≈ 660 rather than 700 ms. Most Model 2B/3 Nelder–Mead runs stop at the
200·n evaluation cap (README D5; 23 % / 29 % converge to tolerance), exactly as MATLAB's
`fminsearch` would with the same defaults; no fitted sigma was negative (D6 never fired).

### C7. Passive session (paper: "Beyond 300 ms, we confirmed the presence of the specific pattern associated with bifurcation dynamics, with a burst of trial-to-trial variability for intermediate SNRs. Compared to the active session, this burst was shifted toward higher SNRs (−7 dB to −5 dB)." — stated for the temporal-electrode ERPs of Fig. 5A and for the projected activity of Supplementary Fig. 4; the passive model comparison is not in the main text)

Decoder trained on no-sound vs the dataset's highest level (6 = −5 dB for S1–S10, 7 = −3 dB for S11–S20,
as the Methods describe — README D14 for the alternative reading of the script). Group profiles
(`figures/fig_a_window_profiles_passive.csv`; level 7 from 10 subjects only):

| 250–300 | −0.46 −0.41 −0.39 −0.28 −0.04 0.29 0.58 | −0.016 −0.038 −0.017 −0.002 0.008 0.019 | 1.30, 0.44 |
| 300–400 | −0.49 −0.42 −0.39 −0.23 0.05 0.38 0.54 | 0.012 −0.015 0.006 0.041 **0.048** 0.027 | 2.78, 0.13 |
| 400–500 | −0.33 −0.29 −0.26 −0.10 0.07 0.26 0.32 | −0.007 −0.015 0.021 0.014 0.030 **0.038** | 2.43, 0.15 |

* **Mean profiles: reproduced** — non-linear increase with SNR (ANOVA on the differences significant
  from 150–200 ms on, F(3,57) = 4.4 … 16.8, all p-FDR ≤ 0.01), weaker and earlier-peaking than in
  the active session (group max 0.58 at 250–300 ms vs 0.84 at 400–500 ms).
* **Variance burst: not reproduced as a significant effect.** The excess SD is an order of magnitude
  smaller than in the active session (group maximum ≈ 0.05–0.08 vs 0.27), no window's SD profile
  differs significantly across SNR (all F(4,76) < 2.9, p-FDR ≥ 0.13; the sample-by-sample map
  `fig_c_variance_burst_passive.png` shows a weak maximum at −5/−3 dB between 150 and 400 ms).
  The *direction* of what little there is agrees with the paper — the largest excess SD sits at the
  high end of the scale (−5 dB at 300–400 ms, −3 dB at 400–600 ms), i.e. shifted toward higher
  SNRs — but the paper's "burst" cannot be claimed from this decoder output.
* **Bayesian model comparison: the bifurcation model does NOT win.** The unimodal non-linear model
  has the highest pxp in every window from 225 to 645 ms (0.58–0.995; > 0.95 at 285, 345, 405,
  465–555 ms); the bifurcation model's pxp never exceeds 0.52 (its maximum is 0.52 at 765 ms, in
  the post-response period where all three models are ≈ 1/3) and its Simes-corrected set is empty;
  its mean ΔLLH against the unimodal model is negative throughout 255–645 ms (−0.03 … −0.40 nats).
  The null model dominates the baseline as in the active session. **Verdict: not reproduced** for the
  passive session — with the caveat that the paper's own passive BMS is only in its Supplementary
  Information, which I do not have, so the comparison here is against the paper's prose claim of
  bifurcation dynamics without report, not against a published passive Fig. 3E.

*Supplementary Information check (Entropy session, 2026-09-11; SI downloaded from the article's
static-content link, 11 pp., saved beside the data as `brain_data/sergent2021/sergent2021_SI.pdf`).*
**The SI contains no passive model comparison.** Its only passive figure, Supplementary Fig. 4, shows
group topographies plus the mean and SD profiles of projected activity over temporal electrodes for
six windows (0–600 ms), with no statistic in the caption; the word "model" occurs in the SI only in
Supplementary Fig. 2's "Models predictions" panel (active session). Two consequences for the verdicts
above: (i) the passive **variance burst** in the paper's own figure is small — excess SD of roughly
0.04–0.08 at −7/−5 dB in the 300–400 ms window, with SEM bands of comparable width — which is the
same magnitude and direction the port finds (0.05–0.08, maximum at −5/−3 dB), so "not reproduced as a
significant effect" should read: *consistent with Supplementary Fig. 4 in size and direction; the paper
reports no test for it, and the port's rmANOVA finds it non-significant*; (ii) the passive **Bayesian
model comparison is not a non-reproduction but a new result** — the paper never ran or reported it for
the passive session, and when run it favours the unimodal model. The paper's "bifurcation dynamics
without report" claim therefore rests on the passive mean/SD profiles and on Fig. 5, not on a model
comparison; for the Entropy study the active (report) session is the reference condition and the
passive condition is exploratory.

*Sensitivity to the training level (README D14).* Re-running S11–S20 with the decoder trained on
level 6 (−5 dB) and the level-7 trials excluded, as the literal script section would have it
(`results/bms_passive_max6.csv`): the unimodal model still wins every window from 165 to 645 ms
(pxp up to 0.98), the bifurcation model's pxp stays ≤ 0.47 within 0–650 ms (maximum 0.45 at 255 ms)
and its mean ΔLLH against the unimodal model stays negative from 285 to 585 ms. The passive result
does not depend on that choice.

### C8. Sensitivity of the active-session model comparison to the decoder solver (README D2, D5)

The whole active chain was re-run with `LogisticRegression(solver='lbfgs')` (today's sklearn
default) instead of `liblinear` (the 2017 default): `results/llh_active_lbfgs.csv`,
`results/bms_active_lbfgs.csv`.

* The decoder output is numerically the same: per-trial preds correlate at 1.0000 between the two
  solvers; mean |difference| 2.3–2.6 × 10⁻³ against a preds SD of 1.1–1.4 (max 0.06).
* The fitted evidences are not: Model 2B / Model 3 test log-likelihoods differ by a median 0.10 /
  0.12 nats per subject-window (95th percentile 0.58 / 0.66, max 5.0), because most of these
  Nelder–Mead runs stop at MATLAB's 200·n evaluation cap and a 0.2 %-of-SD perturbation of the
  input sends them to a different point of a flat likelihood surface. The per-subject-window
  winner (3 vs 2B) agrees in 80 % of cases.
* At the group level the **core of the bifurcation period is stable — pxp(bifurcation) ≥ 0.94 at
  315–525 and 615–645 ms in both runs** (0.999 vs 0.999 at 435 ms) — while its **edges move by one
  to two windows**: with lbfgs the switch is at 285 ms (pxp 0.995 there vs 0.23 with liblinear)
  and the late unimodal period starts at 735 rather than 675 ms (675: 0.81 vs 0.02). Correlation of
  the pxp(bifurcation) time course between the runs: 0.83 over the 53 windows.

**Reading:** the paper's "bifurcation from 250 to 700 ms" and this port's "315–645 ms" differ by
about the amount that the fitting procedure itself is uncertain by. The claim that survives any
version of the decoder is bifurcation dynamics winning decisively between ≈ 300 and ≈ 550 ms in
the active session; the exact onset (250 vs 300 ms) is not resolvable with capped `fminsearch`
fits on cross-validated evidence differences of a few tenths of a nat per subject.

## What I could NOT verify, and why

* **Fig. 2B / the AUC maps and their sign-test contours** — the temporal-generalisation decoder
  (decimated GeneralizingEstimator section) was not ported; it does not feed the model comparison.
* **Fig. 3B/C (heard vs not-heard ERPs and the linear mixed model), Fig. 3F (predicting reports from
  the bifurcation fit), Fig. 4 (cross-session decoding, source reconstruction), Fig. 5C (mind-
  wandering prediction), the control experiments** — outside the brief (the single-trial model
  comparison); their scripts (`SoundConsc_Analyze*Responses.m`, `..._GroupOfElec_Mean_and_STD.m`) are
  in the bundle and could be ported next.
* **Exact numerical identity with the authors' preds/LLH files** — those files are not on OSF; only
  the group-level statistics can be compared, and only against the numbers printed in the paper.
* **The passive session's Bayesian model comparison against a published figure** — the paper reports
  the passive BMS only in the Supplementary Information, which I do not have; the passive result is
  reported here on its own.
* **Which sklearn release produced the authors' decoder output** (README D2) — the sensitivity run
  bounds the effect instead.
