# Reproduction of Sergent et al. 2021 (Nat Commun 12:1149) — single-trial model comparison

Owning document for the port in this folder (pipeline and every deviation: `README.md`).
Run 2026-09-11 by a job agent for the `Entropy` session (RSC Entropy special-issue paper, primary
study T1, human-EEG half); revised the same day after a second-opinion review (Codex) that supplied
the publisher's numerical Source Data and corrected several overstatements — **status: reproduced
with reconciliation** (verdict summary at the end). Data: OSF `aw3t5`, the 20 open subjects, plus the
Source Data workbook (README, "Source Data provenance"). **This is a reproduction of the authors' own
MATLAB/MNE pipeline on their own data, not an independent re-analysis** — where the prose and the code
differ, the code was followed (README D-list).

The whole time course — null baseline, the unimodal-first period, the bifurcation plateau, the
675–825 ms unimodal return and even the post-850 ms structure — overlays the published curve
(`fig_b_pxp_published_vs_port_active.png`). The real differences are (i) at the onset edge, 255–285 ms,
where the published curve already ranks bifurcation highest at 285 (0.886) and the port does so only
from 315, and (ii) in the late interval, 555 and 615 ms, where the published pxp dips to 0.85 and 0.57
and the port stays at 0.99. Multiple-comparison marking: the MATLAB-literal Simes set (strict `<`,
README D7) contains 375, 435, 465, 495, 525 ms; conventional BH adds 345 ms.

**What the group evidence is made of.** The subject-level "log-likelihood" handed to spm_BMS is the
mean over the five folds of the held-out fold's *summed* log-likelihood — ≈ 180 active trials per fold
(`results/evidence_size.csv`). At the plateau (435–495 ms) the bifurcation model beats the unimodal
model in 12–14 of 20 subjects, its Dirichlet expected frequency is 0.84–0.85, and the mean advantage is
ΔLLH(3 − 2B) = 0.43–0.53 nats **per subject per test fold**, i.e. ≈ 0.0025–0.003 nats per trial
(±0.16–0.20 SEM across subjects, t ≈ 2.5–3.0) — neither a per-trial gain of that size nor an ordinary
Bayes factor. The SNR effect itself is established far more strongly: ΔLLH(2B − 0) ≈ 13–18 nats per
fold. The Bayes omnibus risk is ≤ 0.024 from 195 to 795 ms (≤ 0.01 at 315–555 ms).

**Family-sensitivity diagnostic (same saved likelihoods, `results/bms_two_model_active.csv`).**
Dropping the null model and running spm_BMS on {2B, 3} only, the bifurcation model's pxp is 0.62 at
315 ms and 0.86 / 0.82 / 0.81 at 435 / 465 / 495 ms (BOR 0.28–0.38), against 0.96 and 0.999 in the
three-model comparison — the null model, which every subject rejects by ≈ 15 nats, sharpens the
three-way protected probabilities. This is reported as a sensitivity, not as a replacement of the
three-model reproduction, which is the published design.

**Verdict: reproduced under the > 0.95 criterion at the same window (315 ms); the edges of the
interval and the late-interval values differ.** The paper's "after 250 ms" is prose, not a
threshold-crossing estimate; measured against the published curve, both first exceed 0.95 at 315 ms,
the sustained highest-ranked interval starts at 285 (published) vs 315 (port), and both return to
unimodal at 675. Most Model 2B/3 Nelder–Mead runs stop at the 200·n evaluation cap (README D5; 23 % /
29 % converge to tolerance), as MATLAB's `fminsearch` would with the same defaults; no fitted sigma
was negative (D6). The human reference this leaves for the Entropy study is: **a late active-session
preference for the specified mixture model over the specified heteroscedastic unimodal comparator,
with modest incremental predictive gain and sensitivity at interval boundaries.**

### C7. Passive session

What the paper reports for the passive session: the temporal-electrode ERP profiles (Fig. 5A), the
projected-activity mean/SD profiles (Suppl. Fig. 4), the neuro-behavioural correlation (Fig. 5B) and
the mind-wandering prediction from the fitted bifurcation model (Fig. 5C; Suppl. Figs. 5–7 for the
probes, the session-order controls and the tone experiment). **No passive three-model comparison is
reported** in the article or its Supplementary Information; the batch fitting script loops over both
sessions and the paper does fit the passive bifurcation model (Fig. 5C), so the authors ran fits — the
comparison below is an *additional* comparison using this port and its readout, with no priority
claim.

Decoder trained on no-sound vs the dataset's highest level (6 = −5 dB for S1–S10, 7 = −3 dB for
S11–S20; README D14). Group profiles (`figures/fig_a_window_profiles_passive.csv`; level 7 from 10

* **Mean profiles: reproduced** — non-linear increase with SNR (ANOVA on the differences significant
  from 150–200 ms on, F(3,57) = 4.4 … 16.8, all p-FDR ≤ 0.01), weaker and earlier-peaking than in
  the active session (group max 0.58 at 250–300 ms vs 0.84 at 400–500 ms).
* **Variability profile: a modest, readout- and correction-sensitive passive variance effect.** With the
  port's diagonal readout the excess SD is three- to five-fold smaller than in the active session
  (group maximum 0.05–0.08 vs 0.27; note that the two sessions' classifiers are trained independently,
  so their margins are not calibrated against each other), no window's SD profile differs
  significantly across SNR (all F(4,76) < 2.9, p-FDR ≥ 0.13), and the largest excess SD sits at the high
  end of the scale (−5 dB at 300–400 ms, −3 dB at 400–600 ms), the direction the paper describes
  ("shifted toward higher SNRs, −7 dB to −5 dB"). On the **published** subject-level passive SD
  profiles (Suppl. Fig. 4 Source Data, six 100 ms windows, levels −13 … −5 dB — our calculation with the
  port's conventions, `results/published_passive_sd_anova.csv`): 300–400 ms F(4,76) = 4.04, p = 0.0050,
  BH over the six windows p = 0.030; 400–500 ms F = 3.23, p = 0.017, BH p = 0.050; with Greenhouse–
  Geisser correction first (ε = 0.78–0.88) and BH after, the smallest adjusted p is 0.061 (300–400 ms).
  So the published square-averaged profiles carry a passive SD effect that is significant under one
  reasonable convention and not under another, and the port's non-significant diagonal-readout statistic
  neither confirms nor refutes it (D9).
* **Model ranking: the unimodal model ranks highest at 225–645 ms** (pxp 0.58–0.995; > 0.95 at 285,
  345, 405, 465–555 ms); the bifurcation model's pxp stays ≤ 0.52 (maximum 0.52 at 765 ms, in the
  post-response period where all three models are ≈ 1/3), its mean ΔLLH against the unimodal model is
  negative throughout 255–645 ms (−0.03 … −0.40 nats per fold), and **no passive window meets the
  across-window Simes rule** for any model. The ranking holds with the level-6 training variant
  (README D14; `results/bms_passive_max6.csv`: 2B highest 165–645 ms, bifurcation ≤ 0.47 within
  0–650 ms). It is, however, boundary-sensitive: in the refit diagnostic (C8) the 435 ms window's
  unimodal pxp falls from 0.78 to 0.53 and the mean Δ(3 − 2B) changes sign (−0.03 → +0.03), while
  465/495 ms stay at 0.99 for the unimodal model; and in the two-model comparison the unimodal pxp at
  465 ms is 0.755 rather than 0.995 (`results/bms_two_model_passive.csv`).

**Verdict: the port does not reproduce, in the passive session, the active session's late preference
for the mixture model — the specified unimodal comparator ranks highest at 225–645 ms without any
window passing the across-window rule.** This is a statement about a winner among the two fitted
families under this port's readout; it does not establish the physiological absence of state
switching in passive listening. Before any mechanistic reading, two analyses are needed and were
not done here: (i) model-recovery simulations — whether overlapping components, or a rarely occupied
high state, produce the same ranking under this likelihood family — and (ii) a paired active–passive
comparison of the mixture model's predictive advantage per subject. The passive session stays visible
as a challenge condition for the Entropy study.

### C8. Optimiser sensitivity (README D2, D5) — a diagnostic, not a bound

Two runs characterise how the evaluation-capped Nelder–Mead fits behave; they describe this port's
optimiser, not the authors' historical implementation.

*Decoder solver.* The active chain re-run with `LogisticRegression(solver='lbfgs')` instead of
`liblinear` (`results/llh_active_lbfgs.csv`, `bms_active_lbfgs.csv`): the per-trial preds are the same
to 0.2 % of their SD (r = 1.0000; mean |Δ| 2.3–2.6 × 10⁻³, max 0.06), yet the Model 2B / 3 held-out
log-likelihoods differ by a median 0.10 / 0.12 nats per subject-window (95th percentile 0.58 / 0.66),
the per-subject-window winner (3 vs 2B) agrees in 80 % of cases, and at the group level
pxp(bifurcation) ≥ 0.94 at 315–525 and 615–645 ms in both runs while the edges move: with lbfgs the
switch is at 285 ms (0.995 vs 0.227) and the late unimodal period starts at 735 rather than 675 ms.

*Lifting the cap* (`refit_diagnostic.py`; `results/refit_diagnostic_summary.csv`). Each capped Model 2B /
3 fit at 255, 285, 315, 435, 465 and 495 ms was restarted from its end point with 14,000 further
evaluations and the better training likelihood kept:

| variant | window | pxp(bifurcation) capped → extended | pxp(2B) capped → extended | mean Δ(3−2B) capped → extended |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| active | 255 | 0.499 → 0.061 | 0.495 → 0.934 | −0.000 → −0.058 |
| active | 285 | 0.226 → 0.599 | 0.770 → 0.396 | −0.048 → +0.013 |
| active | 315 | 0.963 → 0.991 | 0.034 → 0.007 | 0.119 → 0.137 |
| active | 435 / 465 / 495 | 0.999 / 0.999 / 0.999 → 0.999 / 0.998 / 0.997 | ≤ 0.002 throughout | 0.53 / 0.49 / 0.43 → 0.51 / 0.44 / 0.29 |
| active-lbfgs | 285 | **0.995 → 0.516** | 0.003 → 0.479 | 0.196 → 0.001 |
| active-lbfgs | 315 / 435 / 465 / 495 | 0.982 / 0.999 / 0.998 / 0.996 → 0.981 / 0.999 / 0.999 / 0.996 | ≤ 0.016 | unchanged within 0.06 |
| passive | 435 | 0.208 → 0.460 | 0.781 → 0.529 | −0.028 → **+0.030** |
| passive | 465 / 495 | 0.002 / 0.005 → 0.004 / 0.006 | 0.995 / 0.991 → 0.993 / 0.989 | −0.30 / −0.28 → −0.21 / −0.24 |

After the extension 91 % of Model 2B and **70 % of Model 3 fits** reach the tolerance — ≈ 30 % of the
mixture fits still hit a cap — and the base refits reproduce the committed likelihoods to 5 × 10⁻⁴.
**Reading:** the 315 ms onset and the 435–495 ms plateau of the active comparison do not depend on the
cap or the solver; the 255–285 ms edge and (to a lesser degree) the 555–645 ms edge do, in both
directions. The passive ranking at 465–495 ms is likewise stable, the 435 ms window is not.
