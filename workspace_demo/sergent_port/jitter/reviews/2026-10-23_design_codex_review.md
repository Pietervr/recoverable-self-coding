## Findings ranked by severity

1. **Blocking — the decision is neither exclusive nor sufficient for “DISAPPEARS.”** SURVIVES overlaps framing failure; DISAPPEARS overlaps power failure. An interval containing zero does not establish loss of an advantage. The causal attribution in §9 is stronger still. See DESIGN §6, lines 124–135; §9, lines 188–198; question 4 below.
2. **Blocking — B3 and G3 do not differ only in state count.** The B-only low-state offset and the different dose-response mean families permit a win from baseline or mean-shape fit. The claim of matching in lines 87–90 is false as written. See DESIGN §4, lines 75–98; question 1.
3. **High — the calibration repeats the audit’s principal limitation.** Well-specified generators alone do not test realistic graded alternatives outside the fitted family. Fifty replicates cannot certify the asserted 5% false-call ceiling, and two amplitude settings cannot define a power boundary. See DESIGN §7; `paper/results.tex:88`, `:90`, `:151`, `:158`; question 5.
4. **High — the recovery gate does not establish what “identifying jitter” implies.** It measures recovery of a population spread under G3, with an ambiguous pooled correlation; it neither identifies individual trial latencies nor excludes shape variability being called jitter. Its failure/fallback logic conflicts with the stated stop rule. See DESIGN §7(a), lines 150–167; question 2.
5. **High — the likelihood and fitting specification has unresolved sources of apparent identification.** Spline boundaries, a redundant shape scale, exact zero-jitter handling, covariance misspecification, and held-out information in inherited starts need resolution. 6. **Medium — the cost arithmetic is conditional on an unmeasured implementation and an incomplete manifest.** The quoted arithmetic is approximately correct for 10,000 datasets, but the settings do not unambiguously total ten, and necessary validation is missing. ## 1. Fairness of nuisance matching — remove confounds, declare restrictions

**Baseline and the free low-state offset c — remove the asymmetry.** G predicts exactly `mu0` on no-sound trials; B predicts `mu0 + c` there and in its low branch (DESIGN lines 58, 77, 81–83). Thus B can repair baseline estimation error, low-response offsets, or drift that G cannot. A scalar c is not a second state’s response waveform; it shifts every window, including baseline. If c is intended as a vector, the parameter count changes drastically. The minimal primary comparison sets c to zero. Alternatively give both families the same baseline correction, applied consistently to every branch and estimated with the same training-only procedure. Retain a physically motivated nonzero low-state response only in an explicitly matched extension. Merely declaring this asymmetry does not support “only one versus two states.”

**Dose-response means — match their expressive capacity.** G has one baseline-centred logistic mean. B has mean `(1-A(k))*c + A(k)*m_H(k)*E[T(t-tau)]`, with an independently shaped high-state logistic plus delta, sharing x0 with occupancy. A product of logistic functions plus an offset is not the centred logistic in G. The high-state grading is not mathematically constrained to be “modest.” B can therefore win by fitting level means more accurately without needing informative within-level separation. Conversely, sharing x0 imposes a particular B restriction. A useful primary specification gives both families a free positive mean response for each sound level, with identical constraints, then parameterizes B’s high amplitude so its mixture has that same mean, e.g. `m_H(k)=a(k)/A(k)` when c=0. Occupancy bounds and weak identification near zero must be specified and calibrated. A smaller common mean family is also possible. Preserve the inherited mean laws as a declared sensitivity, not the sole identifying contrast. Do not equalize parameter counts by adding meaningless parameters.

**Template estimation — separate fitting is reasonable; equal construction is essential.** Estimating T within each model avoids fixing a template estimated under the winner (lines 59–63). Do not force the same fitted T on both as the primary solution. Use identical basis, support, normalization, smoothness penalty or bounds, and training-only initialization. It is not literally a shared fitted component, despite the heading at lines 56–57. Model-specific optima remain part of a predictive family comparison; plot their shapes and check a common independently estimated template as a diagnostic if the contrast depends on them.

Several template details must be fixed, rather than just declared:

- The shifted evaluation range is about **−435 to 1425 ms**, whereas knots cover 0–1300 ms. Define zero extension, boundary tapering and any extrapolation. Cubic extrapolation can manufacture early or late signal. A smooth nonnegative basis need not vanish at its endpoints, and zero-phase filtering can place signal before stimulus onset.
- Define T as the already filtered, window-averaged response, or apply the same filtering and 16-sample averaging operator to the continuous response before evaluating its mean. Point evaluation at the centre is not automatically that average (`fit_models.py:138`).
- Normalizing a positive spline by its maximum leaves a redundant common scale in its raw coefficients. Multiplying all positive coefficients by a constant changes no prediction after normalization. Remove that gauge; otherwise the requested Hessian-condition check is structurally singular. The maximum also creates nonsmooth points when its location changes.
- Specify positivity/order constraints on amplitudes, logistic slopes and the B high/low response. Unconstrained inherited coefficients do not automatically define an ordered positive response model. Softplus positivity of T alone is insufficient.

**No-sound anchoring — retain symmetrically and declare its scope.** Forcing `A(no sound)=0` and `a(no sound)=0` is a defensible common anchor after fixing c. It excludes spontaneous response-like events above the no-sound distribution in both models; it is not an empirical discovery of their absence. A free-catch-occupancy sensitivity is useful if this anchor drives the result. Resolve the indexing first: lines 53–54 say levels are shifted by −1, but lines 77 and 81 anchor **1**. In shifted coordinates the catch level is **0** (`fit_models.py:17`, `:89`). Implementing the displayed formulas literally with shifted data would be wrong.

**Parameter counts — correct them and rely on held-out performance plus recovery.** As displayed, B0 has **six** nonspline parameters `(kappa,x0,c,L_H,kappa_H,delta)`, B3 eight, not seven and nine. The inherited seventh parameter was sigma, now fixed through Sigma. G0/G3 have three/five. A normalized spline has one fewer identifiable degree than its positive coefficient count. More mixture parameters are not by themselves unfair: held-out scoring charges their estimation cost. They do mean “same nuisance terms” is not “same flexibility,” and recovery must show that a B win is not just improved mean fit. Do not append AIC/BIC penalties to an already cross-validated score.

**Latency distribution and grid — shared restriction, requiring numerical checks.** The same grid, truncation and quadrature accuracy should be used in both families. It is legitimate that tau is meaningless when B is in the no-response state; do not add a fictitious low-state latency simply to match counts. However, latent spread is estimated separately in each family, and a ±150 ms cap can selectively hurt the family needing longer tails. Declare that restriction and test wider support on prespecified cases. At nominal `s_tau=80 ms`, the continuous truncated distribution has SD **68.13 ms**, not 80; density weights on the 31 stated nodes give **69.30 ms**. Report both the parent-normal parameter and the realized SD, and specify quadrature weights. At very large parent spread the bounded distribution approaches uniform, making the parent parameter weakly identifiable. Refining 31 to 61 nodes and extending the support must change the score contrast much less than the inferential margin. A fixed 10 ms node grid is especially poor near zero; implement exact zero and a verified small-spread limit, not only a log parameter that can never reach zero.

**Mean-one lognormal scale — retain and declare, then stress-test.** Fixing E[g]=1 separates amplitude from scale dispersion and is fair in both response branches. It does not guarantee adequate quadrature in a heavy tail: seven GH nodes need comparison with higher orders over the allowed `s_g` range. Independence of g and tau, constant distributions across sound levels, and a single shape shifted as a whole are substantial shared restrictions. They can disadvantage the families differently under misspecification. Test level-dependent latency and correlated amplitude/latency in a small adversarial battery. A continuous amplitude distribution can also put substantial mass near zero; preference for one of these distributions is not identification of a physiological state count.

**Shared Sigma — retain as a primary working model, declare and challenge its assumptions.** A common fixed matrix is fair in construction; the assertion that template misfit enters “equally” (line 69) is incorrect. Equal weighting does not give unequal mean families equal ability to absorb a residual. Question 3 gives the required checks.

## 2. Identifiability of latency at this readout

**Recoverable in principle as a distributional parameter; individual trial recovery is not assured.** A 10 Hz low-pass is not a 100 ms quantization of latency. A sufficiently strong smooth waveform can carry sub-cycle timing information. The 30 ms grid samples the retained band adequately; 10 ms sampling may improve numerical representation, but does not create independent observations or restore removed frequencies. Specify the sensitivity endpoints: the primary centres −285…1275 ms span 1,560 ms, giving 157 points at 10 ms, whereas 161 points require a wider span. Approximately 900 trials help estimate a distribution; they do not make each trial strong. S10 has only 152 highest-level trials and 152 catch trials, with roughly 80% in any training fold. Weak graded responses and B’s low-state trials carry little or no latency information.

For a known template with unknown trial amplitude, the local Gaussian information for tau, after projecting out amplitude, is approximately

`I_tau|a = a^2 * [T'^T Sigma^-1 T' - (T'^T Sigma^-1 T)^2 / (T^T Sigma^-1 T)]`.

Its inverse square root is an optimistic timing-error scale. Information comes from the slope component distinguishable from amplitude, not from the number of windows. A broad plateau has little timing information. With T itself estimated, broadening of T competes with jitter; scale adds covariance in the T direction while small jitter adds covariance approximately in the T′ direction. Their separability and the noise in those directions matter.

RESULTS C2/C3 gives a substantial highest-level mean difference, about 1.64 decoder units at 400–500 ms, and threshold excess SD up to 0.274. C6 gives a **model-score advantage**, 0.0025–0.003 nat/trial, not a measure of single-trial timing SNR. These summaries cannot certify 40–80 ms recovery. Nor does 40–80 ms jitter necessarily explain a preference throughout a long late plateau: the proposed mechanism is strongest at transitions. That is precisely why §7(b)(i) is valuable rather than predetermined.

**The §7(a) gate is inadequate, not uniformly too strict or too lenient.** A correlation pooling 0/20/40/60/80 ms can be high despite poor calibration at each value. Within one fixed value, “correlation between true and estimated” is undefined unless other true values vary. Define the aggregation and do not treat folds or repeated realizations of one participant as independent participants. A single median can conceal failure in many participants. The 15 ms zero-jitter cutoff is an arbitrary resolution requirement; under an otherwise adequate predictive comparison it may reject useful population-level modelling, while a misspecified zero-jitter generator can still pass the current well-specified test.

Add these before interpreting fitted jitter:

1. Participant-specific profiles of the training marginal likelihood over `s_tau`, jointly reoptimizing T, scale and amplitude at each value, in **both** families. Include zero explicitly; show ridges with `s_g` and template width. Boundary cases need simulation, not a routine chi-square likelihood-ratio reference or a Hessian in log spread.
2. Bias, RMSE, interval coverage and boundary-hit frequency at each spread and noise/response-strength stratum. Recover `s_g` jointly, including zero scale and high scale, rather than varying tau at only one fitted scale. Define how many participant conditions must be informative for a group-level jitter claim.
3. A **zero-jitter graded generator with realistic width/component/shape variability**, plus a signal-noise covariance perturbation. Measure spurious fitted jitter and false state calls. This distinguishes “a shift model fits” from “latency shifts were identified.”
4. If reporting individual tau estimates, test posterior uncertainty, error and calibration against true trial tau on high- and near-threshold trials. Do not require accurate individual tau merely to use a well-calibrated marginal predictive comparison, and do not imply individual recovery from recovery of `s_tau`.

The fallback needs rewriting: lines 154–155 promote G2/B2 after failure, but lines 166–167 say a failed gate stops the real-data step. A scale-only result does **not** close the jitter gap. Predeclare it as secondary, or state that the primary jitter question is unresolved. Weak spread identification need not automatically invalidate family prediction if decision recovery remains good, but it forbids attributing a change specifically to recovered latency variation.

## 3. Residual covariance

**Ledoit–Wolf on training catch trials is a sensible primary working covariance.** It avoids fitting away signal residuals under either competitor and regularizes a highly correlated vector. Training catch counts are only about 120 for S10; at W=161 the empirical covariance is necessarily rank deficient. Estimating a full unrestricted signal covariance separately by level would be unstable. Reestimate mu0, shrinkage and Sigma in each synthetic training fold exactly as on real data; do not hold their generator values known during recovery.

It is not established that catch covariance equals signal covariance. The decoder is fitted independently at each time sample, so noise variance and correlation can vary over time; sound, report preparation, block state and residual components can change them further. If actual signal covariance has additional variance along T, T′ or another component, fitted scale, jitter, or occupancy can absorb it. Underestimated variance makes such structure too influential in the Gaussian score. Overestimated variance can hide real state separation. **There is no model-independent direction of bias in B3−G3.** Depending on its shape, extra variance can favor G’s continuous tail, B’s mixture, or neither. The identical matrix does not cancel unequal quadratic residual errors.

Required checks are held-out catch whitening diagnostics and level-stratified signal residual second moments, followed by at least one prespecified common-covariance sensitivity and simulated misspecification. Check baseline-mean uncertainty as well: estimating mu0 induces prediction error shared across trials in a fold, which the independent-trial likelihood does not explicitly model. Participant-level inference is preferable to pretending those trials are independent inferential units.

Alternatives and fairness costs:

- **AR(1):** cheap, but a stationary AR(1) is a poor automatic representation of a high-order zero-phase low-pass and time-varying decoder variance. A heteroscedastic `D R(rho) D` version is more credible. Fit it once on the same training catch data and use it for both families. This preserves matching while imposing a stronger covariance assumption.
- **Low-rank plus diagonal:** captures a few smooth correlated noise directions, is estimable with shrinkage, and supports Woodbury calculations. Choose rank and regularization from training catch data or prespecified simulations, not the winning real contrast. Use the same matrix in both families and test sensitivity to rank.
- **A common signal-aware covariance:** possible using a model-neutral training residual construction or independent data, but a covariance estimated under G or B can favor its source model. State the reference and test that dependence.
- **Per-model Sigma:** legitimate for a broader predictive comparison if both get the same covariance family and training procedure, but it changes the question. Each model may explain away its own template/state errors as covariance. It is a sensitivity, not a free repair to a comparison claiming only state count differs. Model/state-specific covariance and full dense estimation also enlarge the calibration and compute burden.

## 4. Decision rule and bridge to the inherited result

**The four readings overlap and do not cover failed or invalid fits.** A positive B3−G3 lower bound with a nonpositive B0−G0 lower bound satisfies SURVIVES and framing failure. A B3 interval containing zero with positive B0 and failed power satisfies DISAPPEARS and power failure. Calibration failure and unavailable fits have no coherent place once the fallback/stop conflict is included. Commit a mutually exclusive precedence rule and tests of its cases before real scoring.

**Use an interval on the mean paired per-trial score advantage, with the participant as unit.** This is a good primary estimand for new participants under this protocol and training size. Equal participant weighting is appropriate; folds share training data and are not extra sampling units. A t interval with 20 participants is defensible as a working interval if calibrated and accompanied by individual deltas and influence checks. A participant bootstrap can be a sensitivity, not an automatic guarantee. BMS remains descriptive: `model_comparison.py:36` passes predictive scores to `bms.py:51`, whose input convention is model evidence. Its protected probabilities do not replace this interval.

Fix the normalization first. DESIGN §5 averages fold sums and then divides by scored trials; §6 divides S again. If `S=(1/5)*sum_f LL_f`, the correct per-trial score is `5*S / n_total_scored`, or `S / mean_f(n_test_f)`. The clean definition is **sum every out-of-fold trial log-score once, then divide by total scored trials once**. Define S accordingly. Exclude block 21 from that denominator. A factor of five preserves a sign but invalidates reported magnitudes, margins and power calibration. The inherited score convention is explicit in `fit_models.py:254` and `paper/methods.tex:29`–`:34`.

**Non-significance cannot mean disappearance.** A B3−G3 interval of, for example, `[-0.01,+0.02]` is compatible with a substantial positive advantage. Simulated 80% detection under one alternative does not convert that interval into evidence of absence. For sign-based inference use positive, negative and unresolved intervals. If “no material residual advantage” is the scientific question, predeclare an acceptable positive advantage epsilon on the **joint** score scale and require an appropriate upper confidence bound below epsilon; equivalence within ±epsilon is a stronger, distinct claim. Do not import a single-window 0.003 nat/trial as the same effect in a 53-dimensional joint density. Report both sign and practical magnitude where relevant.

For attenuation, estimate the paired quantity `D_s = Delta_s(B0-G0) - Delta_s(B3-G3)` and its participant interval. “Significant before, non-significant after” is not a test of D. Also show G3−G0 and B3−B0, and the latency-only/scale-only improvements where attribution is attempted. Power should be evaluated at predeclared scientifically relevant joint-score alternatives, not at the noisy observed point estimate after seeing the result.

**B0−G0 is a useful continuity diagnostic, not sufficient reproduction of the published result.** The published M2 has its own fitted baseline and mean-dependent residual SD at every window (`fit_models.py:67`–`:75`); G0 does not. M3 fits sigma per window; B0 fixes a catch-derived matrix. The joint models also impose one response shape, a trial-persistent state and cross-time covariance, replacing 53 separate distributions. The whole epoch includes early graded processing and later report-related activity. Thus a negative B0−G0 could be scientifically informative rather than proof the study “cannot speak” to the original question; a positive value alone does not validate continuity.

Keep the unchanged inherited per-window result as the first bridge. Display held-out per-window marginal scores/predictions of the new no-nuisance models beside it, then the joint B0−G0 result, then the matched-nuisance result. This reveals whether loss of preference already occurs on changing the mean/variance families or coupling time. Freeze an interpretable late-span analysis as well as the full-epoch result; a whole-epoch preference cannot be labelled a replication of a 315–650 ms phenomenon without this check. Treat bridge status and calibration availability as separate flags on a three-way primary interval result, rather than forcing every fact into the present four labels.

Finally, inherited `fits_active.csv` starts contain all-trial initialization information (`README.md:234`–`:245`). Recompute starts using only the current training blocks. The decoder/likelihood folds remain nonnested on these saved preds; this limits an otherwise useful conditional reanalysis and must remain explicit. It is not fixed by training-only Sigma.

## 5. Calibration battery: minimum changes and economical cuts

**R=50 is a development screen, not the asserted licence.** Exact one-sided 95% binomial bounds illustrate the issue:

- 0 false calls in 50 gives upper bound **5.82%**; 2/50, which passes the stated ≤5% observed-rate gate, gives **12.06%**.
- Dropping to 25 makes even 0/25 compatible with **11.29%**. Do not make this the first cost cut for final validation.
- 40/50 detections, the empirical 80% gate, has lower bound **68.44%**. 45/50 has lower bound **80.12%**.
- At a single setting, 0/59 or at most 1/100 can support a 5% upper bound at 95% confidence. Bounds across multiple settings require a declared simultaneous or pointwise interpretation.

Use a finite manifest of generator settings, primary error/detection targets, Monte Carlo precision, and pass conditions based on uncertainty, not just observed proportions. A two-sided 95% interval whose lower bound must exceed zero has nominal 2.5% positive-tail rejection at a regular zero-mean boundary; say whether that is intended. “False two-state” under a graded but misspecified generator is a **generating-family classification error**, not necessarily a type-I error for zero expected predictive advantage (`paper/results.tex:88`). Both classification performance and interval calibration near the predictive boundary matter.

The minimum scientific battery needs:

1. **Well-specified recovery** for zero and 40/80 ms jitter crossed with low/meaningful scale variation, in both G and B, retaining some realistic intermediate/weak participant conditions. Include component overlap and low high-state occupancy. B3 already has a jittered high branch in the design, but only at fitted values; deliberately test jittered ignition and independent early/late timing, rather than assuming that one fitted B3 validates them all.
2. **An out-of-family graded negative control:** variable width or smoothly varying early/late component weights, including a zero-latency-jitter condition, with no discrete classes. A full-rank noise perturbation or empirical residual resampling should challenge the catch-Sigma assumption. Include a level-dependent latency or amplitude-latency coupling case. These can be a small prespecified adversarial set; exhaustive factorial expansion is unnecessary.
3. **Boundary and weak-alternative calibration.** Choose two-state separation/occupancy combinations by their achievable predictive gain against the best graded comparator, using independent large test draws, not simply half an amplitude. Two amplitude points neither guarantee a monotone score effect nor locate the 80% crossing. Include a near-collapsed mixture or another setting near zero expected advantage, and check the participant interval against an independently estimated procedure-level reference with its uncertainty acknowledged.
4. **The inherited-pipeline mechanism test, fully defined.** For §7(b)(i), specify whether a call means any window over 0.95, a sustained plateau, the literal Simes plotting convention, or another fixed summary; these are not interchangeable. Report timing and effect sizes, not just a binary call. Generate no-jitter/latency-only/scale-only controls from matched base parameters so their difference isolates the added mechanism. The inherited Simes-on-pxp convention has no established frequentist error control (`paper/results.tex:46`).
5. **Numerical validity.** Predefine retries, convergence standards and an unavailable outcome when neither competitor has a trustworthy solution. Include every attempted dataset in reported failure counts. Keeping a finite but materially underoptimized fit can create an apparent family effect; flags alone do not license inference. Tolerate optimization/quadrature error only when it is demonstrably small relative to the score contrast.

Generation also needs clarification. Each synthetic participant must have one coherent generating distribution across all five folds, with the real level/block counts. “Parameters fitted to each training fold” does not specify how to generate a single dataset. Refit all training quantities within each simulated fold. If the inferential unit is new participants, resample participant parameter/template configurations or use a declared population generator across replicate sets; rerunning trials for the same fixed twenty configurations checks a conditional experiment, not all population-interval properties. Preserve or stress-test block dependence where feasible.

The design specifies a distribution on 53 processed window values, whereas the inherited entry point expects 1,250 samples followed by filtering and averaging. Specify whether simulation generates full-rate traces and applies that operator, or generates processed vectors and enters the inherited likelihood-fitting stage directly. Do not filter an already processed synthetic covariance a second time. The 161-point sensitivity needs the corresponding joint generator, not independent interpolation noise.

**Cuts that preserve the main licence:** use R=25–50 for development and independent, larger validation only at the retained hard settings; fit only G0/B0/G3/B3 in decision-calibration replicates; reserve G1/G2/B1/B2 fits for mechanism/recovery subsets and real-data attribution. Parameter recovery alone can use one prespecified training-sized fold per replicate instead of five correlated copies, while decision validation retains the full five-fold pipeline. Deduplicate identical generators explicitly; retain zero/40/80 ms before adding a dense recovery grid. Defer passive-session and 161-point full batteries until the active primary comparison is numerically and scientifically viable. None of these cuts justifies removing the out-of-family graded control or reducing final validation precision to R=25.

“All gates evaluated before real contrast” can mean fitted empirical generators are constructed without inspecting the held-out **contrast**, not that real data have never been touched. State that distinction, freeze development choices, and validate on new simulation seeds. Do not select the generator battery or outcome margin after looking at the real advantage.

## 6. Does it close the SI gap?

**Yes, potentially as one central empirical contribution, with revised claims.** The current contribution list (`paper/introduction.tex:25`–`:30`) and claims paragraph (`:34`) already distinguish the partial human reproduction from the transferred-predictor simulation audit. The 989 false calls concern a different sampling unit, fitting scheme and decision rule (`paper/results.tex:70`, `:88`); they do not establish that human latency jitter caused a published error. The human reanalysis plus §7(b)(i) is a substantive way to connect that caution to the human reference. It should replace the “untested jitter” limitation with a bounded answer, rather than simply become a fourth item in an already diffuse list.

Defensible statements after the conditions above are met:

- **Positive matched contrast:** “In this auditory dataset, the two-state family retained a positive held-out advantage over the specified graded family after both incorporated trial-level latency and scale variation.” Give the participant interval, scope of recovery and tested misspecifications. This does not establish that the original result rests on no artifact, as DESIGN §9 currently suggests, or that every graded account fails.
- **Reversal or equivalence with attenuation:** “The inherited preference was not robust to a comparison that represented trial variability; the residual advantage was [negative / bounded below the declared meaningful margin]. Graded simulations with [specified variation] also reproduced [specified features] of the inherited preference.” This establishes a sufficient statistical alternative, not that physiological ignition is absent or that latency is the uniquely established cause.
- **Broad interval, weak recovery, or failure of the bridge:** report that bounded failure as such. A negative or unidentifiable jitter result is useful methodological evidence, but it does not automatically close the proposed human-side mechanism gap.

§7(b)(i) may show that jitter alone does **not** reproduce the late plateau. Report that just as prominently. Then SURVIVES can still be robustness to a specified nuisance, but §9’s statement about the very kinds of variation “that make the transferred test fail” needs its own evidence. If scale alone earns the improvement, do not title the finding an identification of jitter.

A hostile reviewer’s strongest attacks will be: a better competing mean law rather than a state test; noise/shape variability mislabelled latency; a different whole-epoch question substituted for the original late-window result; weak or post hoc absence/power claims; a generator battery built entirely from the fitted families; inherited decoder leakage; and novelty claims overlooking existing single-trial work. Another important limit is the **time-varying diagonal decoder**: `beta(t)^T x(t-tau)` generally differs from shifting the already projected trace, `beta(t-tau)^T x(t-tau)`. Thus a shift of decoder distance is not automatically the latency of one neural source. Declare that readout-level interpretation, and include realistic shape variation as its minimum challenge. A fixed spatial-decoder check would strengthen physiological interpretation, but need not expand the initial task into a complete re-decoding project.

## 7. Literature and Sergent et al.’s own controls

The general latency-amplitude ambiguity is established. I verified the following sources or their primary abstracts/text; their relationship to this precise question differs:

- **Woody (1967), “Characterization of an adaptive filter for the analysis of variable latency neuroelectric signals,”** Medical & Biological Engineering 5:539–554, [doi:10.1007/BF02474247](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02474247). Foundational latency alignment, not a consciousness bimodality test.
- **Truccolo, Ding, Knuth, Nakamura & Bressler (2002), “Trial-to-trial variability of cortical evoked responses: implications for the analysis of functional connectivity,”** Clinical Neurophysiology 113:206–226, [primary article record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11856626/). Amplitude/latency variability can change response statistics. This supports the hazard, not priority for the specific P3b bimodality question.
- **Sekar, Findley & Llinás (2012; online 2011), “Evidence for an all-or-none perceptual response: single-trial analyses of magnetoencephalography signals indicate an abrupt transition between visual perception and its absence,”** Neuroscience 206:167–182, [record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22020091/), [full-text sections 1.5.2.8, 2.3.6–2.3.9 and 3.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4077720/). A particularly relevant precedent: tests latency variance and Woody-corrected averages while assessing all-or-none visual responses. The ambiguity runs partly in the opposite direction—jitter can make an all-or-none event look graded in averages. The authors acknowledge insufficient single-trial SNR to settle invariant amplitude unambiguously. This is not the same likelihood comparison on auditory decoder marginals.
- **Navajas, Ahmadi & Quian Quiroga (2013), “Uncovering the mechanisms of conscious face perception: a single-trial study of the N170 responses,”** J Neurosci 33:1337–1343, [doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.5012-12.2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23345210/). Single-trial amplitude and latency-corrected averages attribute a noise-related average-amplitude effect to latency jitter. Relevant direct consciousness evidence, but N170 rather than P3b or the present two-family test.
- **Rutiku, Aru & Bachmann (2016), “General Markers of Conscious Visual Perception and Their Timing,”** Front Hum Neurosci 10:23, [primary full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2016.00023/full). Explicitly discusses trial latency jitter spreading or hiding NCC and studies N200/P300 timing variability. It also finds amplitude fluctuations in unseen trials important for estimated onset variation. It is not a direct demonstration that jitter alone generates P3b bimodality.

## 8. Compute and cost: 30 seconds is plausible only after the right reduction

**No benchmark supports 30 seconds yet.** For 900 trials, Q_tau=31, Q_g=7 and W=53, evaluating dense quadratic forms separately for all residuals requires approximately **5.49e8 multiply-accumulate terms per objective**, before autodiff and optimizer iterations. The residual array alone has about 10.35 million doubles, about 83 MB, before intermediates. Many simultaneous workers amplify memory traffic and compilation cost. JAX gradients do not remove this work. Conversely, these counts do not prove 30 seconds impossible on modern CPUs; the objective’s algebra makes much of it unnecessary.

With fixed Sigma, Cholesky-whiten the data **once per fold**. For each shifted template `v_tau = Sigma^(-1/2) T_tau` and whitened observation `u_i`, use

`q(i,tau,g) = ||u_i||^2 - 2*g*a(k_i)*(u_i dot v_tau) + (g*a(k_i))^2 * ||v_tau||^2`.

Recompute the 31 whitened templates when spline parameters change; evaluate all trial-template projections with one matrix multiplication. This requires about **1.48e6** trial/template/window products plus template whitening per objective, and scalar operations over the 217 quadrature nodes. It is exact for the stated model, avoids the large residual tensor, and keeps a dense common Sigma. Compute the B low branch separately; sum its two branches with log-add-exp. Cache basis evaluations at all tau nodes and group amplitude calculations by level. This is the first optimization, before replacing a well-performing covariance or reducing quadrature accuracy.

**EM over z** is optional. The Bernoulli state is already summed analytically; EM does not remove the tau/g integrations and can converge slowly near overlapping states. Joint responsibilities over `(z,tau,g)` can provide useful starts, followed by converged gradient optimization, but introduce another algorithm requiring recovery. **Woodbury** is useful if a validated low-rank-plus-diagonal Sigma is chosen; it is unnecessary merely to avoid repeatedly solving 53-dimensional dense residual systems. **Coarser grids** are development tools until their score error has been bounded; cutting GH nodes or tau support first may change the answer. A Gaussian small-jitter/derivative approximation is a different model and cannot silently replace 40–80 ms shift integration.

Use JIT with cached shapes, avoid recompilation at every start, constrain BLAS/XLA threads explicitly, and measure steady-state **value plus gradient and convergence from all four starts**, not a single forward pass. Record compile time separately and measure completed-dataset throughput under realistic concurrency, including retries. Synchronize timing as described in [JAX’s benchmarking guide](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/benchmarking.html). Use float64 and verify log-score/gradient accuracy against a small independent implementation; the inherited gain is small. Four clustered starts at 0.25 perturbations are not evidence of finding all important basins—include materially different widths/spreads and a near-collapsed B solution in development.

**Budget arithmetic and a workable reduction.** At the design’s assumed prices, which I did not independently verify as live regional quotes, 10,000 datasets ×160 starts ×30 seconds is 13,333 core-hours: about $236 at $1.7/instance-hour or $736 at $5.3, assuming perfect 96-worker utilization. AWS documents [c7a.24xlarge as 96 vCPUs and 192 GiB, with one vCPU per physical core](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/c7a/); that supports the nominal concurrency, not perfect scaling.

The manifest needs an exact count: §7(a) says fitted plus five jitter settings, §7(b) five generators, §7(c) two. Before explicitly justified reuse this is thirteen settings, and twelve if the fitted G3 case is shared. Zero-jitter G3 and G2 are identical only when **all** generator parameters are identical, not when independently fitted templates happen to share a label. Include inherited per-window fitting/BMS, sensitivities, validation additions and retries; do not cost only joint fitting.

For illustration, **12,000 synthetic participant-datasets ×80 starts** (the four primary/control models) ×30 seconds is 8,000 core-hours, about **$142** at the assumed $1.7 rate. At 70% effective utilization plus 25% reserve this is about **$253**; the same assumptions allow roughly 47 seconds per start before reaching $400, before separately budgeted additions. This is a conditional budget envelope, not a price guarantee. It shows why cutting unnecessary model fits before cutting validation replicates is effective. Keep a hard measured budget for recovery/adversarial additions, checkpoint complete dataset results for spot retries, and retain the design’s launch/spend approval step. If the final validation cannot fit the cap after these reductions, narrow the claimed licence or stop; an under-calibrated “DISAPPEARS” is not an economical success.
