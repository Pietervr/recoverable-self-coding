## 1. Is the matching sufficient, including the occupancy floor and second moment?

**Yes at the level of model-family construction: attainable means and nuisance restrictions now match.** This does not equalize within-level variance flexibility or identify a physiological state count. For common parameter values of `a`, `T`, and the latency/scale distributions, put `v=T_tau`, `b=E[v]`, `H=E[v v^T]`, and `r=E[g^2]=exp(s_g^2)`. The exact continuous-model moments at a sound level are

```
E_G[y-mu0] = E_B[y-mu0] = a b
Cov_G[y] = Sigma + a^2 (r H     - b b^T)
Cov_B[y] = Sigma + a^2 (r H / A - b b^T).
```

Thus `m_H=a/A` removes the extra *mean-law* route to a B win. Separately fitted shapes/spreads need not give identical fitted means; the important correction is equal attainable mean construction. The two occupancy parameters now also provide a level-dependent second-moment law. At zero jitter/scale, G0 has no signal variance and B0 has signal variance `a^2(1/A-1)` along the template. At `A=.02`, the scalar amplitude variance is `49 a^2`; at `s_g=1.5`, it is approximately `473.39 a^2`, versus G's maximum `8.49 a^2` at that scale bound. This is intended mixture flexibility, not the old unmatched baseline error, but it must be declared. A B win can reflect useful variance/tail modelling without visibly resolved modes. With scale variation B's high branch is continuous: its amplitude law is a point mass at zero plus a lognormal high branch, not a two-point distribution (lines 111–112). The lognormal variance bound can itself matter to the comparison; flag G fits at that bound and prespecify a targeted wider-scale sensitivity if they drive the result, with adequate quadrature.

**The floor is a numerical/model restriction, not a treatment of identification.** It bounds `m_H/a` by 50 and limits the rare-tail construction. Near the floor, occupancy and the largely unseen high response remain weakly determined; near `A=1` the mixture collapses. Hard clipping makes occupancy derivatives zero in clipped regions and introduces a kink at the threshold. Logging activation is necessary but insufficient. Prespecify occupancy/high-amplitude profiles or comparable diagnostics and a floor sensitivity, for example .01/.02/.05 on the real comparison and hard development cases. Archive which levels clip. If a smooth lower-asymptote logistic is chosen instead, document that it changes the occupancy family; do not silently substitute it for `max(.02, logistic)`.

No arbitrary parameter-count equalization is needed. G0/G3 = 5/7 and B0/B3 = 7/9 nonspline parameters are now correct generically. Catch anchoring is symmetric. The free-catch sensitivity still needs equations for **both** families and a baseline/covariance estimation rule that does not automatically absorb the freed catch signal. A B-only free occupancy is not the promised common sensitivity.

## 2. Is 255–705 ms appropriate, and does it create an asymmetry?

**Yes as a frozen, conditional late-response analysis.** The article's stated 250–700 ms span is a defensible external rationale, and the 16 selected centres are correct. It should be described as chosen after the published and reproduced curves were known, before this new contrast. RESULTS C6 and manuscript Results distinguish the article's prose from the actual source curve: its first .95 crossing is 315 ms and both published/port curves return to graded-highest at 675 ms. Do not describe every selected window as an established two-state plateau.

The early-component argument is sound as a *possible framing bias*: a B low branch fixed at catch throughout a whole epoch cannot represent an early sound response shared by low- and high-state trials. G can represent an always-present response, although its own single-shape restriction also makes the whole-epoch task misspecified. This does not guarantee a G win. The late restriction applies identically to both likelihoods and introduces no new B-only mean term. It does discard timing information and does not establish that common graded activity is absent after 250 ms. In particular, the first window averages 240–270 ms, and the zero-phase filter does not provide a sharp stage boundary. The conclusion remains about a catch-anchored late-response family, not arbitrary two-state physiology. Keep the whole-epoch sensitivity as a different, informative framing.

**There is, however, a new structural consequence.** Primary window samples cover 240–720 ms. With the stated latency nodes, every evaluated template argument lies in 90–870 ms. Many spline basis functions on -150–1450 ms are therefore completely unseen, even after setting two coefficients at each end to zero. Giving the unseen coefficients more of the softmax mass and multiplying all `a(k)` by the compensating factor leaves every primary prediction exactly unchanged. This reintroduces a scale degeneracy despite coefficient sum one. It also makes `a(5)` an unsuitable response-strength stratum until the normalization is repaired.

I checked a conventional open-clamped cubic basis with interior knots every 60 ms: 30 coefficients, 26 free after the four endpoint zeros, and **10 free coefficients identically absent from all primary evaluations**. Halving all visible coefficients, reallocating their mass to those ten, and doubling all amplitudes produced exactly identical predictions. The precise count depends on the knot convention, which is not yet specified; the unobserved-support problem does not. Remove/fix invisible coefficients and use a normalization tied to the identifiable template, or specify another identifiable continuation/constraint. Recheck rank at exact zero spread and for the wider-support sensitivity. A softmax also retains an additive-logit gauge unless one logit is fixed or the logits are otherwise constrained; coefficient normalization alone does not remove that numerical redundancy.

This does not show that latency is unrecoverable. It shows that the proposed parameterization has an avoidable exact nonidentifiability before recovery begins.

## 3. Are precedence, epsilon and absence claims now acceptable?

**The finite-interval sign partition is exhaustive and exclusive.** A bound exactly zero belongs to UNRESOLVED. Attenuation as a paired difference is correct, and BMS being descriptive is correct. POSITIVE and an upper bound below a positive epsilon may coexist; they are different questions and both should be reported.

Finish the following operational definitions before branch tests:

- Name the exact VALIDITY gates: final error/power calibration, numerical validity, and primary fit availability. Explicitly exclude the RECOVERY identification flag from this veto. The broad phrase “any §7 pass condition” should not have to be reconciled with the later NOT IDENTIFIED exception by an implementer.
- Use the actual available participant count and its `n-1` degrees of freedom. D requires the intersection available for **all four** G0/B0/G3/B3 scores. Define availability thresholds for secondary intervals, invalid/nonfinite endpoints, and an unavailable epsilon. Do not compute D by subtracting means over different rosters.
- Steps 3–4 and the flags must still be computed after a valid primary sign. The phrase “each step ends the evaluation for that level” needs this explicit interpretation in `decide.py`.

**One third of a model-implied no-nuisance benchmark is an acceptable declared *relative* margin**, not a biological minimum effect. But “expected advantage” needs an estimand. Is it an oracle comparison under a fitted B0 distribution, or the expected score difference of the finite-training-size fitted procedure? Independent large test draws can estimate a test expectation conditional on fitted predictors; they do not integrate over training/refitting variation. Specify the B0 generator fit (full-sample or which training fold), comparator fitting, participant/level weighting, Monte Carlo precision, and what happens if the benchmark is nonpositive or indistinguishable from zero. Freeze its algorithm before development and its estimated value before the real contrast. The manifest currently names G3/B3 configurations, but does not supply this separate B0 reference calculation.

`U<epsilon` is an upper-bound claim about the B advantage, **not two-sided equivalence**. Rename that step accordingly; equivalence within +/-epsilon would require both endpoints. Using the upper endpoint of a two-sided 95% interval is conservative for a one-sided 95% bound, provided that interval is calibrated for its target. The new §6 does not itself infer absence from non-significance.

**§9 still overreaches in two places.** A NEGATIVE B3-G3 result can occur when B0-G0 was already negative and attenuation is absent. Then the new framing favours G; it has not shown that adding trial variation removed the inherited preference. For the stronger “not robust to representing trial variability” wording, use the BRIDGE result and positive D, or explicitly separate framing change from nuisance attenuation. Any claim that graded simulations reproduced named features must also require that they actually did so; it is not implied by a negative real contrast. Finally, failure of the tested latency generator to reproduce a plateau licenses “these tested jitter distributions did not reproduce it,” not line 284's assertion that the plateau **is not** a rising-edge artefact. That is an exclusion beyond this battery.

## 4. Does the manifest license §9, and are the binomial conditions right?

**The two stated numerical bounds are right.** Exact one-sided 95% Clopper–Pearson values are 4.65598% for 1/100 false calls and 80.1167% for 45/50 detections. State that the bounds are pointwise for the frozen empirical generator settings. Do not present them as simultaneous confidence intervals or guarantees for untested populations. Requiring all graded gates to pass is an intersection requirement; Bonferroni is not automatically necessary merely to apply that all-pass rule. The empirical resampling of twenty configurations is a useful population-generator improvement, but remains conditional on that finite empirical distribution.

**The covered decision events remain incomplete.** The false-POSITIVE gate protects one positive-sign event; T1 power measures detection at one alternative. Neither bounds a false conclusion `U<epsilon` when the procedure's true mean advantage is at least epsilon. T3/T4 currently report detection of `L>0`, not erroneous upper-bound decisions, and coverage is only reported at W2/O1 with no validity threshold. Add an explicit calibration of the upper-bound event at the epsilon boundary, including finite-training procedure-level reference uncertainty. Use existing weak-alternative runs to report false NEGATIVE and coverage as well. A near-zero procedure reference is useful for the sign interval. This is the same need to test the claimed interval target that motivated the earlier audit, not an invitation to a large new factorial grid.

A cost-conscious change is to replace one T3/T4 setting with an epsilon-boundary setting and promote it to R=100, evaluating the relevant erroneous decision, rather than adding many strong alternatives. If a 5% upper-error claim is intended, 0/50 still has upper bound 5.8155%; fifty runs cannot certify it even with no errors. Set the chosen precision and pass rule explicitly. Bound uncertainty in the independently estimated reference; do not classify Monte Carlo noise around epsilon as a known boundary. Define how separation/occupancy is varied to reach each target, and an unreachable-target outcome. “Independent large test draws” alone cannot provide an unconditional procedure reference if no independent training/refitting draws are made.

**UNAVAILABLE needs an error-accounting rule.** A graded setting with 100 numerically unavailable sets could currently record 0 POSITIVE/100 and apparently pass. Keeping flags does not prevent that. Either count unavailable outcomes conservatively as possible errors for the error bound, or add a prespecified numerical-completion requirement that prevents licensing on such a setting. Power must count unavailable sets as non-detections. Define whether up to four unavailable participants are handled by the same available-case rule in simulation as on real data, and how that interacts with a set's flag. Include every attempted set in the denominator.

**Repair the controls by replacement, not expansion.** W3's fitted `s_g` is not latency-only; W4's 40 ms jitter is not scale-only; W1's independent G0 fit changes template/amplitudes as well. For the mechanism contrasts, fix one common configuration per participant and change only the intended nuisance. For example redefine W1/W3/W4 as `(s_tau,s_g)=(0,0),(80,0),(0,1)`, with common `a,T,mu0,Sigma`; retain W2's fitted G3 case for joint variation. Then the claimed isolation is true without adding validation settings. Freeze the inherited three-window run rule, including ties and a missing-run value. Retain the strict MATLAB Simes set for continuity. Assigning real block labels preserves counts, not stochastic block dependence; declare that limitation or use a specified block-resampling challenge.

**Cost:** 22,000 participant-datasets and 1.76 million start-level optimizations are correct for the stated final comparison manifest. Recovery is `(18+2)*25*20*8*4 = 320,000` start-level optimizations, or **2,667 core-hours at 30 seconds**, rather than 1,200, before profiles and interval construction. Faster simple models might reduce this, but require measured, model-specific timings. The inherited mechanism subset contains 19,000 participant-datasets: 53 windows * 3 models * 5 folds implies **15.105 million Nelder–Mead runs**, plus BMS on 950 sets * 53 windows. Six hundred core-hours assumes about **0.143 seconds per optimizer run before BMS**. It is unverified, not a measured envelope. With only the corrected recovery arithmetic and the still-assumed 600 hours, the supplied spot rate gives roughly $318 ideal / $567 at 70% utilization plus 25% reserve, before omitted work. These are calculations at the design's rates, not live AWS quotes.

Keep benchmark-before-cost and the declared protection of graded R and O1/O2/O5. Benchmark the inherited pipeline, generator/reference fitting, profiles and uncertainty construction too. Prefer reusing/replacing controls and deferring optional attribution fits over reducing final calibration precision. Any frozen-manifest reduction requires the corresponding fresh-seed validation; the original licence cannot simply carry over.

## 5. Is recovery adequate for “identify jitter,” and does its flag feed §9 correctly?

**The intended separation is now right:** failed parameter recovery need not invalidate calibrated prediction, but it forbids claiming that recovered latency explains it. Both-family recovery, shape controls, profiles, per-setting error and strata are substantial improvements. Forty/eighty-ms tolerance gates can establish a useful conditional resolution for the specified parent spread. They do not by themselves demonstrate that real data contain nonzero jitter.

Make five items explicit:

1. Keep the twenty generating configurations fixed for participant-specific recovery, with R=25 independent datasets **per configuration/setting**. The population resampling scheme used for decision calibration does not automatically produce twenty identifiable recovery conditions. Define “median relative bias” as a statistic over those replicates, the exact RMSE, stratum cutoffs, and whether the **same** fifteen conditions must pass at both 40 and 80 ms. Report each family separately; identification in G does not establish it in B.
2. Define the denominator of the zero-jitter gate. “10% of participant conditions” could mean twenty medians or hundreds of individual estimates; these yield very different false-jitter rates. Report replicate-level false-jitter frequencies within configuration/stratum and their uncertainty, not only thresholded medians. Include the already-generated B3 zero-jitter settings when licensing jitter attribution under B. Specify which zero-jitter scale setting(s) the gate covers.
3. Specify how profile intervals, boundary critical values, bias correction and “recovery-corrected uncertainty” are constructed. Coverage of an unspecified interval cannot be implemented. State which fold/full-data parameter estimate and profile are reported on real participants, so the recovery training size matches their uncertainty procedure. Predefine acceptable coverage if these intervals will support identification.
4. Require real-data evidence: a calibrated profile/interval that meaningfully separates the relevant spread from zero, alongside the recovery flag. A method can pass injected-jitter recovery while estimating zero on the real dataset. Call the flag recovery adequacy, or explicitly state that IDENTIFIED means *identifiable in the tested simulation conditions*, not *jitter detected here*. Resolve the spline degeneracy before interpreting those profiles or `a(5)` strata.
5. Add the existing-model contrasts **G3-G2 and B3-B2** for incremental latency after scale is allowed. `G1-G0` constrains scale to zero and can reward latency as a surrogate for amplitude variability. Combine the incremental result with real profiles and the repaired latency-only control. Even then, the attribution is to a useful readout-latency term under the challenged model, not uniquely to a neural latency mechanism.

NOT IDENTIFIED should feed §9 as planned, without stopping prediction. IDENTIFIED alone must not unlock the stronger attribution sentence. No extra individual-trial-latency gate is needed for the marginal comparison, and no universal physiological identification claim is warranted by this recovery grid.

## 6. What still prevents exact implementation?

In addition to the preceding scientific issues, freeze these concrete choices in the specification/code contract:

- **`models.py`:** the complete knot vector, boundary convention, fixed/invisible coefficients and logit gauge; an identifiable normalization; stable occupancy/log-mixture calculations; and explicit use of `a/A`, rather than `a`, in §5's high-branch quadratic form. Softplus amplitudes attain strictly positive values, not exact nonnegative zero; either declare that closure or provide an exact amplitude boundary where required. Define the common free-catch sensitivity and `D R(rho) D` estimation method/bounds.
- **`fit.py`:** numeric gradient/objective tolerances, parameter scaling, iteration limits, retry/start recipes and their acceptance rule. “Meets neither” in line 140 permits acceptance when just one stated standard passes; say which standards must all pass. Specify how the training level-5 trace becomes positive spline coefficients, how the catch percentile statistic is constructed from a vector, and how spread starts are mapped to models with fixed-zero components. Verify numerical convergence of the selected quadrature rather than assuming 61/15 is automatically adequate after 31/7 fails. Extending +/-150 to +/-250 changes the truncated latent model, not merely integration accuracy; refreeze and regenerate/calibrate accordingly. Preserve parent-versus-realized SD reporting.
- **`simulate.py`:** one explicitly chosen full-data or training-data generator fit per participant, separate from CV score fits. Supply the **53-dimensional** baseline, covariance and template continuation needed for the inherited test. The primary fit supplies only 16-dimensional noise quantities and leaves early/late template coefficients unconstrained; zero-padding, a full-span auxiliary generator, or another extension are scientifically different choices. Do not silently choose one. The inherited first crossing/full curve/Simes set depend on that choice even though the binary run is restricted to the late span.
- **Adversarial generators:** O1's time-stretch centre, normalization and distribution parameters; O2's actual component shapes and Beta parameters; all five O3 level spreads; O4's joint law/correlation before versus after truncation and mean-one scale treatment; O5's reference model, residual definition, centring and vector/block resampling. Empirical residuals can retain the original unknown mixture/response variability, so O5 is not automatically a known no-discrete-state covariance generator. State that limitation or construct a noise challenge with known truth. For T2, specify whether mean amplitudes are held fixed by recomputing `m_H`, and whether halved occupancies may cross .02; halving a logistic occupancy is generally outside the fitted logistic family. Specify T3/T4's parameter path, reference fitting, precision and target-attainment rule.
- **`decide.py`:** separate primary sign, attenuation, upper bound, recovery and bridge fields; the explicit VALIDITY/error-count rules above; no automatic nuisance attribution from NEGATIVE alone. Branch tests should include a failed recovery with otherwise valid prediction, missing B0/G0 despite available B3/G3, zero/nonpositive epsilon, a positive-but-small advantage, failed calibration, and unavailable/nonfinite fits.
- **Freeze sequence:** distinguish generator/development fits from final real-data CV fits. Lines 6–7 permit fitted empirical generators before freezing; lines 180–182 say choices are frozen before real-data fits. Name the stages: provisional implementation/generator fits; development and numerical repairs; frozen code/decision/generator algorithms; independent final validation and frozen margin; final real contrast. Do not inspect the final contrast during development. This removes the apparent circularity without pretending the empirical data were untouched.

These are focused repairs to one coherent study. The shared mean construction, late-span target and sign partition can stand. The next revision should make the generator, numerical validity and jitter-attribution claims as explicit as the primary score now is.
